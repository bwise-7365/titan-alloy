\chapter{Testing and Lessons Learned}
\label{ch:testing}

This chapter is the library's evidence. It describes how the test suite is
built and what each layer of it gates (Sections~\ref{sec:t-infra}--%
\ref{sec:t-protocol}); then the three application problems as case studies
--- the banded flow-planning QP and the deployment game with equal weight,
as the paired monotone-at-scale and nonmonotone stories, with the influence
game between them (Sections~\ref{sec:t-flow}--\ref{sec:t-deploy}); and
closes with the cross-cutting lessons and the known open items
(Sections~\ref{sec:t-lessons}--\ref{sec:t-open}). Wherever a number
appears, its source is named: a section of
\code{network/doc/performance.md}, a dated run log, or a test that
reproduces it.

\section{Test philosophy and infrastructure}
\label{sec:t-infra}

The suite is GoogleTest, fetched by CMake \code{FetchContent} at a pinned
release tag and compiled by the project's own toolchain and flags. At the
time of writing it stands at 111 test cases, all green, exercised by the
aggregate targets of Chapter~\ref{ch:manual}. Beneath GoogleTest sits a
thin shared harness (\code{utils.hpp}): a bound solver is a
\code{SolveFn} (everything captured but the start point), a pass/fail
criterion is a \code{CheckFn} returning a one-line report, and
\code{runCase} runs one solve --- timing it, printing the standard
statistics, reporting a throw as that case's failure rather than aborting
the program --- so every solver test in the library is the same procedure
with different bindings. A test-only bridge (\code{testsupport.hpp}) maps
the harness onto GoogleTest assertions without ever letting GoogleTest leak
into the shipped library.

The suite divides into three layers with different jobs:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Unit tests} pin the building blocks: line searches,
  Levenberg--Marquardt primitives, the FD Jacobian, projectors, the config
  parser, instance generators.
\item \textbf{Protocol tests} pin the \emph{control logic} of engines ---
  rescue paths, stall cutoffs, chain rounds --- by injecting deliberately
  failing or fake components (Section~\ref{sec:t-protocol}).
\item \textbf{Acceptance tests} pin end-to-end behavior on the three
  application problems, gated on author-verified answers
  (Sections~\ref{sec:t-flow}--\ref{sec:t-deploy}).
\end{itemize}

Gates are written to be \emph{honest under multiplicity}. Where a problem
has one verified answer, the gate pins it (converge \emph{and} match).
Where legitimate outcomes vary --- nonmonotone games where individual
engines may honestly fail, or multiple equilibria --- the gate is expressed
over a set: ``at least $k$ of these rows pass'' (with $k$ pinned to the
known-good count so a regression in any known-good row is caught), or
``the answer matches \emph{some} entry of a verified roster.'' A converged
answer that matches no roster entry prints itself in paste-ready roster
form, so charting a newly discovered equilibrium is one verification away
from a one-line addition.

\section{The minimal problems}
\label{sec:t-minimal}

A flock of small constructed-solution problems gives every engine a
fast-running, exactly-checkable workout. Each family exists to gate
something specific:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Constructed LCPs} (\code{makeComplementaryPair}: draw a
  complementary $(w^\ast, z^\ast)$, then set $q = w^\ast - Mz^\ast$). With
  $M = A^\top A$ (PSD by construction) they gate correctness of the
  monotone engines (\code{lcp\_psd\_test}); with random indefinite $M$
  (\code{lcp\_random\_test}) they gate the \emph{divergence guard itself}
  --- the test asserts the throw. An engine that silently ``converged'' on
  the indefinite instance would be the bug.
\item \textbf{Cubic VIs} (\code{makeCubicProblem}: $F = A^\top g(Ax) + k$
  with elementwise cubic $g$, PSD-forced Jacobian, known solution) drive
  the Josephy--Newton loop end to end; the four-engine comparison test
  (\code{han\_vs\_he\_test}) runs an identical LCP and an identical cubic
  VI through every engine row and requires each to recover the known
  solution --- the library's standing cross-engine consistency check.
\item \textbf{Ellipsoid-$K$ problems} gate the claim that the projection
  engines and the driver are generic in $K$: the same VIs solved over
  \code{makeEllipsoidProjector}'s set, plus the projector's own
  root-finding unit tests.
\item \textbf{Degenerate and rank-deficient LCPs} gate the interior-point
  engine's reason to exist: strict complementarity broken at every third
  index, $M = A^\top A$ rank-deficient. Iteration counts 9 / 19 / 10 on
  the clean / degenerate / rank-deficient instances
  (\code{mehrotra\_ipm\_test}) --- tens, not thousands.
\item \textbf{Mixed hand QPs} (small KKT systems with hand-checkable
  solutions) gate the mixed free/orthant plumbing. One of them earned its
  keep during development: a mixed QP whose solution sits exactly on a
  Fischer--Burmeister kink exposed a $y$-space/$z$-space confusion in the
  semismooth kink indicator that \emph{no pure-LCP test could see} (with
  $n = 0$ the two spaces coincide). The fixed solver then solved that QP
  in 2 iterations with residual exactly zero --- the expected signature of
  Newton on a problem that is affine on the active piece
  (\code{semismooth\_newton\_test}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Protocol testing: fakes, not hard problems}
\label{sec:t-protocol}

The suite's most transferable lesson is methodological. Engine
\emph{protocols} --- rescue paths, drift guards, stall cutoffs, chain
control flow --- are triggered by rare numerical events, and hunting for a
natural problem that reliably triggers them is both fragile and, in one
documented case, impossible:

\begin{quote}
The IPM's singularity rescue was long ``covered'' by a test named after
rescuing a singular free block. Probing showed the rescue had \emph{never
fired}: with an exactly singular block, partial-pivot LU leaves a zero
pivot, but the corresponding right-hand-side entry is \emph{structurally}
zero on any consistent system, and $0/0$ evaluated through the
back-substitution yields finite results --- so the non-finite trigger never
trips. Only an \emph{inconsistent} singular system (which the surrounding
algorithm does not produce) throws NaNs. The test was renamed to assert
what it actually shows, and the rescue is now driven the honest way.
\end{quote}

The honest way is \emph{dependency injection of failure}: every protocol
test drives its engine with a deliberately broken or fake component bound
through the same seams production code uses. The failing-factory test
hands \code{mehrotraIpm} a factory whose first factorization returns NaN
and then delegates --- the rescue's regularization sequence is asserted
deterministically. The drift-guard test hands it an \emph{echo} factory
and asserts \code{newtonCheckTol} catches the inconsistency by name. The
Josephy--Newton stall cutoff is proven with a \emph{frozen} inner solver
(returns its start; the outer step is exactly zero, so the residual
provably cannot improve). The alternating chain's control logic ---
best-point memory, throw-as-stall, the improvement cutoff, perturb-restart
--- is pinned with fixed-point, throwing, and identity stages
(\code{alternating\_chain\_test}), including the guarantee that a stagnant
chain with perturbation disabled stops after one wasted round while the
same chain with perturbation enabled spends its full round budget trying.
Fakes make the rare path the \emph{common} path, deterministically, in
milliseconds.

\section{Case study 1: the banded flow-planning QP}
\label{sec:t-flow}

The flow-planning pipeline (Chapter~\ref{ch:manual}; mathematics in
Chapter~\ref{ch:math}) was developed against two instance geometries:
\emph{laydown 0} (random square, near-metric costs --- direct arcs
dominate, screens work) and \emph{laydown 1} (banded rectangles,
near-tied costs --- multi-hop routes, wide flat optimal faces, degenerate
by construction). The second is the stress case, and its 200-node instance
drove three generations of solver work. All numbers below are from
\code{network/doc/performance.md} (sections P1--P9) and the dated run
logs of 2026-07-05/06; the keep-all configuration is reproducible from
\code{network/ns3-keepall.cfg} (seed base 20260704).

\paragraph{Generation 1: projection-contraction + screens.} On spread
geometry the screened pipeline is excellent: the 70-node profile solves in
0.15--0.9\,s with the screen versus 144\,s keep-all ($\sim$160$\times$),
and the certificate loop confirms the screened answer identical to 5--6
digits (P1--P3). On the 200-node \emph{banded} instance the same engine
hits its 150{,}000-iteration cap ($\sim$6 minutes) with no answer at LCP
dimension $\sim$1{,}900 --- the flat-face stall of
Chapter~\ref{ch:math} in the wild. Tightened tolerances, certificate
slack, and tie-break adjustments all failed to rescue it (P4--P5): the
problem was the engine class, not the tuning.

\paragraph{Generation 2: the interior-point engine.} The same instance
under \code{engine = ipm} (dense LU), screened, one certificate round:
convergence in \textbf{35 iterations at Newton dimension 10{,}838} (the
size of the linear system factored each iteration) ---
degeneracy-insensitivity holding at scale --- but 29.6 minutes of wall
time, nearly all of it dense $O(d^3)$ factorization ($\sim$50\,s per
iteration), and an honest \code{certifiedP = false} after the certificate
\emph{balloon} grew the kept set from $\sim$1{,}900 to 10{,}737 of 14{,}500
pairs in a single round (run log 2026-07-06; P6--P7). Verdict: the
iteration-count problem was solved and the cost had moved into the linear
algebra times the screening formulation.

\paragraph{Generation 3: structured linear algebra, no screen.} With the
flow Newton factory (Chapter~\ref{ch:math}) and \emph{no screen at all} ---
all 14{,}500 pairs kept, hence nothing to certify --- the same instance
solves in \textbf{36 iterations and 4.51 seconds}, certified exact by
construction, at Newton dimension 14{,}601 (P8--P9). That is
$\sim$394$\times$ faster than the screened dense-LU path \emph{and}
strictly better: the screened one-round answer had overshot the optimum by
$\sim$0.4\% ($\theta = 53.117$ vs.\ $52.908$). The banded/large production
default is now \code{ipm} + \code{ipmNewton = flow} + no screen; small
spread-geometry instances keep screened \code{bsHe94b}.

Three findings generalize beyond this problem. \emph{Iteration count and
per-iteration cost are independent axes}: generation 2 fixed the first and
was still unusable until generation 3 fixed the second. \emph{Screens
trade optimality silently}: the 0.4\% overshoot came from an honest,
certificate-checked pipeline whose round budget ran out --- exactness by
construction beat exactness by certification on both time and quality.
And \emph{nondimensionalization is mandatory}: the exact rescaling of
Chapter~\ref{ch:math} took the small lane-solve benchmark from 25\,s to
0.04\,s before any algorithmic work at all (P1).

\section{Case study 2: strategic allocation of effort}
\label{sec:t-saoe}

The influence game (Chapter~\ref{ch:math}) enters the suite twice, and the
contrast is itself a result. The library's original reduced formulation
(\code{saoe.hpp}: effort space only, intermediates eliminated) is solved by
the projection engines --- \code{bsHe94b} reproduces the reference
equilibrium. The \emph{literal} GAMS MCP transcription
(\code{gams\_alloceff\_test}: dimension 87, with the intermediates and
shadow prices as explicit variables) is measurably harsher on the same
engines: both projection rows trip their divergence guards within
milliseconds, as the explicit coupling rows make the Josephy--Newton
linearizations indefinite. The two Newton-class engines are untroubled
(run log 2026-07-06):

\begin{itemize}
\item \code{semismoothNewtonSolve}, cold start, FD Jacobian:
  \textbf{14 iterations, 182\,ms}, squared residual $8.2\times 10^{-9}$;
\item Josephy--Newton over the IPM inner: \textbf{2 outer / 16 inner
  iterations, 83\,ms}, squared residual $1.1\times 10^{-11}$.
\end{itemize}

Both land on the \emph{same} equilibrium, which the author confirmed is
the one PATH reaches on the GAMS side --- and which matches the reduced
implementation's reference equilibrium. Two independent engine classes and
two independent formulations agreeing on an author-verified point is the
strongest transcription-fidelity evidence the suite has; the gate is
pinned to it, and pinned at \emph{two} passing rows so neither known-good
engine can mask a regression in the other.

\section{Case study 3: the deployment game}
\label{sec:t-deploy}

The deployment game (Chapter~\ref{ch:math}: dimension 450, nonmonotone by
design, poles just outside the orthant, multiple equilibria) is the
library's nonmonotone acceptance challenge, and it was not solved by any
engine as shipped. It was solved by a \emph{composition} built in eight
short diagnostic runs over one day (2026-07-06); the sequence is worth
recording in full because each run's failure bought the next run's design
--- and because the diagnostic tooling mattered as much as the algorithm.

\paragraph{Runs 1--2: no standalone engine, and why.} On the GAMS interior
start, every engine failed differently. The semismooth solver cut the
squared residual $1.4\times 10^{5} \to 2.1\times 10^{4}$ in 20 iterations
and then died on a failed line search \emph{while still descending} ---
not a plateau. \code{bsHe94b} threw its divergence guard immediately
(expected off-monotone). The Josephy--Newton/IPM row made a spectacular
first step ($1.4\times 10^{5} \to 22.9$ in one linearization) and then
froze at $19.6$ for $\sim$22 outer iterations at $\sim$1\,s each until its
inner IPM threw on a collapsed step length: the linearization had gone
indefinite. Run 2 added the SEMI-style semismooth variants: nonmonotone
memory 4 escaped the iteration-20 death (85 iterations) but plateaued at
$1.2\times 10^{4}$; the $\lambda = 0.95$ restart \emph{visited}
$8.2\times 10^{3}$ and then wandered up to die at $1.7\times 10^{4}$
(the best-visited-iterate lesson); plain FB died fastest, in 10 iterations
--- Chen--Chen--Kanzow's critique \cite{chen2000} in miniature. All cold
semismooth endpoints differ wildly at natural-residual norm $\sim$100: a
landscape of traps, where line-search tuning only changes which trap.
Meanwhile the IPM's stall point was \emph{feasible}, with sensible mixed
strategies, three orders of magnitude closer than any cold semismooth run.
Run 2 also motivated two pieces of permanent infrastructure: the
Josephy--Newton no-progress cutoff, and flushed per-iteration heartbeats
(the first run of this problem was killed after minutes of \emph{buffered}
silence, losing all its output).

\paragraph{Run 3: the failure modes are complementary.} A two-phase chain
--- Josephy--Newton/IPM to its stall, semismooth warm-started from the
stall point --- descended $19.6 \to 0.68$ in 52 iterations, four orders of
magnitude below any cold semismooth run, then plateaued. The new
residual-breakdown diagnostic (print the worst natural-residual rows
\emph{by GAMS name}, with their $y$ and $G$ values) localized the plateau
exactly: negative escort allocations ($-0.34$ against $\epsilon = 0.01$)
--- the iterate camped beside the poles, where the line search dies against
$+\infty$ walls. The obstruction was geometry, not merit stationarity, and
it named its own cure: project back onto $K$ between phases.

\paragraph{Runs 4--5: the alternating chain, hardened, solves it.}
Alternating rounds of project $\to$ globalize $\to$ finish exposed two
robustness requirements in one run: round 2's re-linearized IPM was
\emph{improving} ($1.19 \to 1.07$) when its inner solver threw, and the
escaping exception destroyed a $0.68$ best point --- hence
\emph{throw-as-stall} with best-point memory (and, later, the IPM's
step-length collapse became an honest non-converged return in the library
itself). With those in place, run 5 converged in round 4 to squared
residual $6.0\times 10^{-15}$ in 17.7\,s --- with the semismooth quadratic
tail visible in the log's last three iterations
($3.8\times 10^{-5} \to 7.1\times 10^{-8} \to 6.0\times 10^{-15}$) --- and
the author confirmed the answer matches the GAMS solution.

\paragraph{Runs 6--8: determinism, perturbation, and a second
equilibrium.} Promoting the chain into the library alongside two solver
semantics changes legitimately shifted the trajectory; the new path
reached $0.145$ \emph{in the correct basin} and stagnated --- and runs 6
and 7 produced digit-for-digit identical output, the cleanest possible
demonstration that a deterministic round map at a fixed point cannot be
rescued by any stopping-rule adjustment. The remedy (PATH's playbook
\cite{ferris1998}) is scaled perturbation on stagnation: jiggle the
restart by $U[-1,1]\cdot s\cdot\sqrt{\rho(\mathrm{best})}$, vanishing as
the chain closes in, from a fixed seed so runs stay reproducible. Run 8
converged in round 4 ($1.2\times 10^{-9}$, 28.8\,s) --- to a
\emph{different, equally valid} Nash equilibrium: same strategy supports,
mirror-image probability-cap pattern (Red's cap active where the GAMS
point has Blue's). The acceptance gate became a verified-equilibrium
roster with both entries, and the perturbation acquired a second job:
sampling the equilibrium set, which is the author's stated method for
charting it.

\section{Cross-cutting lessons}
\label{sec:t-lessons}

Nine lessons recur across the case studies; each is stated with its
evidence.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Engine class beats engine tuning.} No amount of
  projection-contraction tuning touched the banded stall; the central path
  is insensitive to the geometry that causes it
  (Table~\ref{tab:banded}). Symmetrically, no semismooth line-search
  variant escaped the deployment traps that composition walked around
  (Table~\ref{tab:deploy-runs}, run 2).
\item \textbf{Structure exploitation beats screening.} The screen
  approximates the problem to fit the solver; the structured factory made
  the solver fit the problem --- faster ($394\times$) \emph{and} exact
  (Section~\ref{sec:t-flow}).
\item \textbf{Composition beats any single engine off-monotone.} The
  chain's value is precisely that its stages' failure sets do not
  intersect at handoff points (Chapter~\ref{ch:math}); no component
  becomes a better standalone solver by being in it.
\item \textbf{Stalls are not errors.} A collapsed step length or failed
  line search at a real iterate is information; throwing on it cost a
  converging run (run 4) and costs every wrapper a catch block. The
  library-wide contract --- bad values throw, stalls return honestly ---
  is a direct export of this chapter.
\item \textbf{Return the best point visited.} Nonmonotone strategies may
  end above their best (run 2's $\lambda$-restart: returned
  $1.7\times 10^{4}$, had visited $8.2\times 10^{3}$); both the semismooth
  solver and the chain now report the best, judged by one fixed
  functional.
\item \textbf{Determinism makes verbatim retries worthless.} Runs 6--7's
  digit-identical outputs proved the round map was at a fixed point;
  scaled, fixed-seed perturbation breaks the fixed point without
  sacrificing reproducibility or convergence.
\item \textbf{Observability is a solver feature.} Flushed heartbeats (the
  buffered-silence incident), named residual breakdowns (which found the
  poles), and honest \code{converged} flags turned each failed run into
  the next run's specification. None of it costs anything when disabled.
\item \textbf{Gate on verified answers, structured for multiplicity.}
  Pinned answers where unique (both case-study gates), pinned
  \emph{counts} of passing rows where engines legitimately differ,
  self-extending rosters where equilibria multiply
  (Section~\ref{sec:t-infra}).
\item \textbf{Process: verify algebra before code; inject failure to test
  protocols.} The flow factory's Maxima script \cite{maxima} found sign
  conventions before any C++ ran; the fake-component method
  (Section~\ref{sec:t-protocol}) is how every guard in the library is
  actually exercised.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Known limitations and open items}
\label{sec:t-open}

Recorded honestly, with their planned dispositions:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The IPM cannot warm-start} (structural: it needs a strictly
  interior, near-path start). Re-solve loops that would benefit from warm
  starts use the projection engines or pay full IPM price.
\item \textbf{Dense $M$ at scale.} The engines hold $M$ densely for
  residual matvecs; keep-all at the 200-node profile costs
  $\sim$1.7\,GB and $O(d^2)$ per residual. A matrix-free linear-operator
  seam for $M$ is the noted generalization, out of scope to date.
\item \textbf{\code{dHan06}'s refactor-per-iteration cost} ($\sim$12$\times$
  \code{bsHe94b} in wall time at equal iterations on the SAOE runs) is
  accepted; cache/reuse across $\beta$ updates is a recorded option if the
  engine ever matters at scale.
\item \textbf{SAOE equilibrium selection remains open}: on the reduced
  formulation, all tested starts fall into one basin that is not the
  reference equilibrium; the test gates on feasibility. The alternating
  chain (with its perturbation sampler) is the natural candidate and has
  not yet been pointed at it.
\item \textbf{Comparative benchmark rows} (chain / \code{bshe94b} /
  \code{ssn} bounded probes on the 200-banded instance) are planned as
  report data; they gate no decision.
\item \textbf{The engine dispatcher} (\code{chooseEngine} over problem
  features) and the \textbf{acceleration wrappers} (Anderson/Halpern) from
  the engine roadmap are designed but unbuilt.
\item \textbf{More PPD versions are coming}; each arrives as data plus a
  \code{VIModel} builder and a new roster, per the recipe of
  Chapter~\ref{ch:manual}.
\end{itemize}
