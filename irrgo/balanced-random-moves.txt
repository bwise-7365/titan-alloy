The game of Hex is very computationally challenging.
However, random-move Hex has a surprisingly simple
strategy when each side has 50/50 chance of playing the next move.

Of course, it is well-known that the "gambler's fallacy" really is false.
There is no tendency to 'make up' past uneven outcomes.
Naturally, this makes me wonder how to make it always somewhat biased toward
a more even outcome, i.e. to slightly favor the player (X or Y)
who has not had many moves lately.

P(X=1, Y=0) = (CY + 1)/(CY + CX + 2)
P(Y=1, X=0) = (CX + 1)/(CX + CY + 2)

where updates occur by
CX <-- X + f*CX
CY <-- Y + f*CY

e.g. f = 0.9
and CX = CY = 0 initially.


OR we could just say
P(this X = 1 | previous Y = 1) = 0.60
P(this Y = 1 | previous X = 1) = 0.60

Either seems to be very even over 100 moves or so.
