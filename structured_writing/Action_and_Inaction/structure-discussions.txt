Part 1 — Key ideas, one line each

The US can destroy almost anything it can locate; mastery of action is not in question.
Bomb-damage analysis is the right yardstick for acquiring a weapon, the wrong one for strategy.
Strategy inherits the acquisition metric because revisiting it would indict the original choice (paradigm-protection).
Luttwak's levels: technical and tactical success do not climb to the operational or strategic on their own.
Paradoxical logic: the cleaner the strike, the worse the strategic result can be.
Vietnam proved twice that overwhelming technical superiority does not win wars.
The loser's veto: a war ends only when the weaker side chooses to lose — extermination being a genocide the stronger won't commit — so the decision is the weaker's.
Endurance beats action: against a side that will not quit, destruction accumulates but never converts to victory.
Force acts on the body; it cannot reach the will, where the decision lives.
The power of inaction: a withholdable benefit makes stopping, not striking, the operative threat.
Dependency is the strategy of inaction — engineered need turns withholding into leverage.
Mild economic dependency (gains from trade, relative advantage; Hirschman) binds only at the margin.
Strong economic dependency is hollowing out: atrophy capacity and control follows — British (machines), Chinese (cheap labor), Saudi (commodity monopoly plus rentier favor-seeking).
Athenian military dependency: monetize a partner's security until it has neither skill nor funds to act (Athens at sea, Sparta on land).
British military dependency: install an unpopular regime, then meter its insecurity so it needs the patron against its own people.
Threatened inaction is a directed network, not one reversible arrow — edges coexist, and cycle where both need the trade.
Versailles (Taylor): void an agreement by non-performance, forcing the other to acquiesce or wage a futile war.
Iran is Germany toward Washington and France toward Beijing; the bombs never touched either pillar.
Two lines of effort: keep creating an assailable flank (action) and keep building dependencies (inaction).

Mapping the 19 into a latent vector space, then clustering
You asked for technically respectable but not exorbitant. Three contrasting routes, with the tradeoff that matters at n = 19.
A — Lexical embedding. TF-IDF (or a count vector) over the 19 lines → cosine distance → Ward/average linkage → dendrogram.

Pro: fully offline, deterministic, reproducible in ten lines of scikit-learn; a real vector space with zero model dependence.
Con: the conceptual kinships here are almost entirely non-lexical — "force reaches the body, not the will" and "endurance beats action" share no content words — so it will cluster on incidental shared tokens (dependency, action, Iran) and mislead. Respectable, but the signal is too thin for this corpus.

B — Neural sentence embeddings. SBERT (e.g. all-MiniLM-L6-v2) or a hosted embedding API → cosine → agglomerative, with UMAP or MDS for a 2-D map.

Pro: captures semantic similarity, the modern standard, a few lines of code, yields both a latent map and a tree.
Con: the model weights (HuggingFace) and the API hosts are outside this sandbox's allowed domains, so I cannot run it here — you would run the script locally (MiniLM is ~90 MB, CPU-fine). And at n = 19 the embedding geometry is dominated by a few directions; cluster assignments are unstable to model choice, and silhouette/cophenetic diagnostics are noisy. The right tool, somewhat fragile at this scale.

C — Elicited dissimilarity → classical MDS → Ward (recommended). Build a 19×19 dissimilarity matrix D from structured pairwise judgments; double-center −½·J·D²·J and eigendecompose (classical MDS = PCA on the Gram matrix) for a latent Euclidean embedding X; keep top-k dimensions by the scree; Ward-link X for the recursive dendrogram; report cophenetic correlation for tree fidelity and plot the 2-D MDS scatter as the concept map.

Pro: delivers exactly what you described — a latent vector space (the MDS coordinates) and recursive clustering (the tree) — runs now, offline, in numpy/scipy; the subjectivity is fully localized in D, which is inspectable and editable cell by cell; n = 19 is ideal for this rather than too small.
Con: D encodes judgment, not an independent measurement. And the honest trap: if I seed D from the same affinity reasoning I just used by hand, C will agree with the hand-clustering by construction. Its value is the machinery — the editable matrix, the scree and cophenetic diagnostics, a perturbation/jackknife check for cluster stability, and a 2-D map you can argue with — not an independent oracle.

My recommendation: C now as the workbench (I hand you an editable D, the dendrogram, the 2-D map, and the cophenetic/stability numbers), with A run alongside as a cheap orthogonal sanity check, and B as a local script if you want a model-independent embedding to break ties where C and A disagree. If genuine independence matters more than convenience, invert that: B is the oracle and C becomes the cross-check.
Method references I'd build on: Torgerson (1952) and Gower (1966) for classical MDS; Ward (1963) for the linkage; Sokal & Rohlf (1962) for cophenetic correlation; Reimers & Gurevych, Sentence-BERT (EMNLP 2019, arXiv:1908.10084) for B. I can pull verified open-access links for any of these.
