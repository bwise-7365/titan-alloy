
The Supreme Court has long held that three things cannot be patented: laws of nature, natural phenomena and abstract ideas. Mathematical formulas and bare algorithms count as abstract ideas. The reasoning is the one your RAND lawyers gave: such things are discovered, not invented, and must stay free for everyone to use (the Court’s own example is that Einstein could not have patented E=mc²). An invention that applies a formula to a practical end can still be patented, as the Court allowed in 1981 for a rubber-curing process that used a well-known equation. Since Alice in 2014, however, claiming “the idea, but done on a computer” is not enough; the claim must add something genuinely inventive beyond the idea itself.
