READING THE OVERLAY
- Every hex shows its id twice: the PRINTED id (black, small, just inside the hex's BOTTOM edge) and OUR id
  (magenta for the tile's core hexes, orange for margin hexes) near the hex's TOP. They name the same hex.
  A black id sitting just above a hexside belongs to the hex ABOVE that hexside.
- Thin magenta lines are our grid; they lie on the printed grid. Small magenta dots mark hexside midpoints.
- Flat-topped neighbours: n and s are in the same column (row -1 / +1); ne, se, sw, nw are in the next or
  previous column. Columns with an EVEN printed number (02, 04, ...) sit half a hex LOWER than odd ones.
- The tile's AREA = core + margin hexes (all hexes with a magenta or orange id). Record every hex of the
  area, and every hexside or link step whose BOTH hexes are in the area. A hexside on the printed map edge
  (a hex with no neighbour beyond it) is written "HEX:DIR", e.g. "0120:nw".
- Name an inner hexside by its two hexes, "0419-0420" (order does not matter).

DECISION RULES FOR HARD CALLS
1. Terrain: a hex takes a terrain only if it covers at least HALF of the hex. The draft's coverage numbers
   are measured by colour masks and are more exact than an eye estimate. Between about 0.35 and 0.65 THE
   MEASURED COVERAGE DECIDES: keep the draft terrain, and just delete the draft's "unclear" entry. Override
   the draft only when the mask counted the wrong thing (a swamp's hatch or green text counted as woods, a
   lake outline, a blob the mask plainly missed) or the coverage is far from what you see (off by more than
   0.2); then add a note "terrain HEX: mask says woods 0.52, but ...".
2. A line passing exactly through a hex vertex shared by hexes A, B and C, going from A on one side to C on
   the other: step A-C (A and C are neighbours), NOT A-B-C, unless the line runs visibly inside B for a
   stretch (more than about a fifth of the hex width).
3. A line clipping only a small corner of a hex (less than about a fifth of the hex width inside it) does
   not enter that hex. The draft marks such cases "only a corner of X holds the line": usually correct.
4. A junction (a line branching off another) is in the hex where the branch point is printed, even when a
   city is next door. Say so in a note.
5. A line running ALONG a hexside for a stretch does NOT cross that hexside: never record the two hexes on
   either side of it as a step. Record the chain of hexes on ONE side of the line (the side its continuation
   enters), and add an "unclear" entry at the first such hexside with both options in the note. Example: a
   road running level along the 5415|5416 and 5615|5616 hexsides and through 5516 from corner to corner is
   5316-5415, 5415-5516, 5516-5615, 5615-5716 (or the 54xx16/56xx16 side), never 5415-5416, 5515-5516 or 5615-5616.
6. A river along a hexside is on that hexside even when it wobbles 10-15 px into one hex. A river that
   cuts through a hex interior from one vertex to another: record the two hexsides that together are
   closest to its course, with a note.
7. Road and rail are separate kinds: a road on top of a rail is recorded as both.
8. If you cannot decide from the tile, make a focused crop (3x, one hexside or hex):
     python crop.py pgg primary work/pgg/look/T-A-B.jpg --side A-B --radius 0.8 --scale 3 --grid
     python crop.py pgg primary work/pgg/look/T-HEX.jpg --hex HEX --radius 1.2 --scale 2.5 --grid
   (--grid adds our overlay; omit it to see the print alone.) If it is still undecidable (under a label,
   too faint), leave the feature OUT of the lists and put an "unclear" entry with "at", "feature" and a
   "note" saying what you see. "at" is ONE hex or hexside token ("3901" or "3901:n" or "0419-0420"); put the
   options you are choosing between in the note ("3901:n or 3901:nw"). Every remaining "unclear" entry must be a real question, not a leftover
   from the draft: delete draft "unclear" entries you have resolved and record the resolution in "notes".
