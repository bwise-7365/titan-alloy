READING THE OVERLAY (same on both tiles)
- Every hex shows OUR id in magenta (core hexes) or orange (margin hexes) near its TOP. On the scan the printed
  id (black) sits near the hex's BOTTOM; on the render the renderer's id sits near the bottom too.
- Name a hex by its id, a hexside or a link step by its two hexes "A-B", a map-edge hexside "HEX:DIR".
- Check the core and margin hexes (all hexes with a magenta or orange id).

WHAT TO COMPARE, hex by hex (the render is schematic: that is not a difference)
- terrain: the render fills a whole hex with one terrain (woods dark green with dots, swamp pale green
  hatched, lake cyan, clear pale). The print draws blobs. It is a difference only when the hex's terrain in
  the render disagrees with the half-hex rule on the print (woods/lake/swamp covering at least half of the hex
  -> that terrain; less -> clear). Swamp: a hex holding most of a printed swamp hatch symbol is swamp. Woods:
  a small blob drawn (almost) wholly inside one hex is that hex's forest symbol: the hex is woods even when the
  blob covers less than half of it. Eye estimates of cover run high: when a blob looks close to half the hex,
  read the MEASURED cover from the image, work/pgg/candidates.json -> "hexes" -> HEX -> "woods" (or "lake"), with
  "woods_blob" (the share of its blob inside this hex, 1.0 = a one-hex symbol). Report a terrain difference only
  when that measure (plus any railway or road pixels crossing the blob) disagrees with the render, or when the
  mask plainly missed the blob. Borderline hexes already looked at are in work/pgg/verify/exceptions.json.
- place: the renderer draws a major city as a pale grey hex with 4-5 scattered black rectangles (buildings),
  and a town as a small white square. HOW a city is drawn (its buildings versus the print's many small grey
  blocks, their number and positions) is a deliberate style, NOT a difference. What must match is WHETHER
  a hex holds a city or town, its kind (major city or town) and its name: every hex holding printed city
  blocks must be a render city hex, a printed town ring must have a render town square, and no render city
  or town may sit where the print has none. Name labels may sit elsewhere: not a difference.
- rivers: the render draws rivers along hexsides with rounded corners. A difference is a printed river on a
  hexside the render leaves empty, or a render river on a hexside where the print has none.
- rail (white dashed line on black) and road (grey line): the render draws them hex centre to hex centre
  (smoothed through hexside midpoints). The print draws straight lines that cross hexes off-centre. A
  difference is a hexside the printed line crosses that the render's line does not cross, or the reverse.
  A printed line through a hex vertex counts as crossing into the hex it continues in (not the one it touches).
- Ignore: exact line positions inside a hex, label positions and fonts, colours, the render's own hex ids,
  the scan's margin furniture (brackets, boxes outside the map), the compass rose, the dotted light-blue
  limit line between columns 40 and 41, and blue set-up lines in column 01.
- A railway or road that leaves the printed map across an edge hexside is drawn in the render only as far as
  that edge hex's centre (the sheet language has no off-map link). That short stub is NOT a difference; a
  missing line inside the map is.
- The named exceptions in work/pgg/verify/exceptions.json and work/pgg/catalogue/resolutions.json (entries with
  "exception": true) are decided on purpose (for example a road that runs ALONG a hexside, drawn on one side of
  it). Do not report them again.
