# The Russian Campaign, Deluxe Fifth Edition (GMT/Consim Press 2022) -- rulebook digest

Source files read: trc.txt (main rules, read in full); trc-ref.txt (revised and reformatted rules, read through 28.3 and compared); three fan aids converted with pdftotext (rules summary v1.03, game-turn housekeeping, CRT/SOP/replacements reference); map images, for the CRT, Terrain Effects Chart (TEC), weather chart, Airpower Availability Chart and sea boxes. The Movement Allowance Chart (MAC) and Order of Battle (OB) displays were not available.

## 0. Rulebook structure
1.0 Introduction; 2.0 Units and Markers; 3.0 Prepare for Play: Campaign Game; 4.0 Sequence of Play (4.1 Axis Player Turn, 4.2 Russian Player Turn, 4.3 Sudden Death Victory Check); 5.0 Movement; 6.0 Stacking; 7.0 Zones of Control; 8.0 Movement Restrictions (8.5 Kerch Strait); 9.0 Rail Movement (9.1 Overview, 9.2 Capacity, 9.3 Mechanics, 9.4 Conversion); 10.0 Sea Movement; 11.0 Headquarters and Leaders; 12.0 Combat; 13.0 Battle Resolution; 14.0 Terrain Effects on Combat; 15.0 Air Power: Stukas and Sturmoviks; 16.0 Automatic Victory Attacks; 17.0 Supply (17.2 General, 17.3 Combat); 18.0 Russian Paratroops; 19.0 Partisans; 20.0 Reinforcements (20.1 Off-Map Units Box to 20.10 Off-Board Encirclements); 21.0 Axis Replacements; 22.0 Russian Replacements; 23.0 Motorization; 24.0 Axis Minor Allies; 25.0 Victory Conditions (25.1 Campaign, 25.2 Sudden Death); 26.0 Optional Rules (26.1-26.17); 27.0 Scenarios (27.1-27.6); 28.0 Bonus Materials (variants); 29.0 Developer Notes; credits; index.

The book runs about 34 pages, with decimal cases to three levels (e.g., 9.4.4). Rules 1-25 are the "classic" game, ordered roughly as movement, rail, sea, combat, air, supply, then reinforcements, replacements and victory; 26-28 are optional rules, scenarios and variants. Illustrated examples sit beside the rules. The CRT, TEC, weather and airpower charts are printed on the map; the MAC is on player aid cards; reinforcement schedules are on the OB displays. The reformatted version keeps the numbering but adds lettered subparagraphs and tables.

## 1. Situation and design
- Situation: the German-Soviet war from Barbarossa (June 1941) to Berlin (May/June 1945), historical, with Finland, Rumania, Hungary and Italy as Axis minors.
- Scale: two-month game turns, each player turn split into two impulses (about one month each; 4.0, 20.0). Hex size is not stated. Axis units are mostly corps; Russian infantry are armies and Russian armor and cavalry corps; HQs are army groups and the Stavka. Two players.
- Scenarios (27.0): campaign, May/June 1941 to May/June 1945 (25 turns); Breaking Storm (5 turns, to Jan/Feb 1942, a three-hour tournament game); Barbarossa (10 turns); Fall Blau (8 turns from May/June 1942); Zitadelle (6 turns from May/June 1943); Bagration (7 turns from May/June 1944). Scenario victory counts VPs against a start line (major cities and oil wells 2, minor cities 1; 27.12). Campaign victory (25.1): the Axis wins by holding Moscow and killing Stalin, or if the Russians lack Berlin at the end; the Russians win at once on taking Berlin. Sudden death (25.2): after each Jan/Feb turn, a side holding all of that year's objectives wins (1942: Kiev, Kalinin, Leningrad, Rostov, Kharkov, Stalino).
- Course of play: the introduction recounts the history (fast advance, Kiev diversion, failure at Moscow). Scenario notes say the Axis holds the early initiative and the Russians strike back as weather turns; 26.15 notes an Axis ploy of sparing Russian units so the Russians lack replacements.
- Options: 17 optional rules (26.x), including industrial evacuation, battlegroups, artillery, fortress cities, strategic movement (doubled movement away from the enemy), historical weather, naval limits, bidding for sides in Russian replacement points, and mobile leaders. Section 28 adds untested variants (Hitler no-retreat, extra OB units, Warsaw uprising, "But What If" strategic alternatives, political chits).
- Design (29.0): the developer stresses continuity with the 1974/1976 design. Changes: rail junctions (to stop rail converting ahead of the front), sequenced exchanges (to settle city and rail control), two Guards/armor replacements late in the war, and promotion of Sturmoviks and off-board encirclement to standard rules. The cumulative weather DRM damps extreme weather runs (4.1.1); gauge conversion models the logistical lag of the advance (9.1).

## 2. Map
- Hex terrain (TEC): clear, woods, mountain and swamp each cost 1 MP. Units stop on entering woods, mountain or swamp, except that infantry-type units ignore woods, mountain units ignore mountains, and swamp is clear in snow (8.2). Mountains and major cities double the defender (14.1). In woods, attackers ignore AR/A1 retreats and defenders ignore DR/EX/D1 retreats (14.2).
- Rivers run through hexes, not along hexsides. A defender is doubled "behind a river" when every attacker stands on a river hex and the defender is not on the same river (14.1.1); not for the Russians on the opening impulse (14.1.3). Doubling never compounds.
- Linear features: railroads (no roads at all); country and military-district borders (setup, opening attack limits, 3.7); prohibited hexsides (no movement or ZOC); impassable black coastlines except the Kerch Strait, where units stop after crossing, ZOCs do not cross, attacks are voluntary and defenders are doubled (8.5). One rail segment is rail-only (8.6).
- Other features: major and minor cities (supply, control, worker sites); rail junctions (controlled like cities, 9.4.2); three oil fields (Ploesti, Maikop, Grozny); Black Sea and Baltic ports; Archangel (lend-lease); friendly edges (west for the Axis, east and south for the Russians).
- Boxes and tracks: Turn Record Track (0-25, also holding DRM, worker RP, invasion and bid markers); weather chart and box; replacement pool and surrendered boxes for each side; Off-Map Units Box; Stukas and Sturmoviks boxes; Paratroop Reserve; sea boxes.
- Markers (2.0): weather, weather DRM, railhead, RR Convert, RR Move, invasion, sudden death, worker RP, para range; optional fortress city and barrage. No supply or disruption markers; control follows occupation and ZOC.

## 3. Units
- Kinds: combat units (infantry, armor, motorized, cavalry, mountain, paratroop, Luftwaffe, SS, Guards; optional artillery and battlegroups); Russian workers (immobile, with combat and replacement value); 3 partisans; HQs (3 German army groups, the Stavka); Hitler and Stalin; Stuka and Sturmovik air units, which are odds-shift tokens.
- Counts: the rules give no totals. Stated: 3 Axis HQs, 1 Stavka, 2 leaders, at most 3 Stukas and 3 Sturmoviks, 3 paratroop corps, 3 partisans. Per-side counts cannot be derived from the text.
- Counter: nationality color, type, size, identification, combat factor (attack and defense alike) and movement factor (first impulse, clear weather); the back gives the setup area or arrival turn (2.1-2.6).
- Steps: none. A unit is eliminated (to the replacement pool) or surrendered (gone for good; 13.2).

## 4. Movement
- Land: 1 MP per hex; allowances vary by impulse, weather, nationality and type per the MAC (5.4), which is not in the text. The text shows most Russian infantry cannot move in the second impulse (26.10), only HQs move in a Mud second impulse (10.1 example), and snow appears to halve allowances rounded up (inferred from the 26.10 example). HQs move only in the second impulse, at full allowance whatever the weather (11.1); leaders move only by rail.
- ZOC (7.0, 8.3-8.4): rigid. Units stop on entering and may not move ZOC to ZOC; a unit starting the second impulse in an enemy ZOC cannot move. Partisan ZOC covers only its own hex.
- Stacking (6.0): two armies, three corps, or two mixed; HQs, workers and leaders are free.
- Rail (9.0): first impulse only. Axis 6 units per turn (3 in snow), Russians 5, plus all first-impulse reinforcements and replacements free. Unlimited distance over contiguous friendly rail, never in or through an enemy ZOC; no other movement that impulse. Off-board rail links friendly edge exits. Conversion: in the end phase the owner advances railhead markers to the farthest rail hex his units reached, if a ZOC-free rail path back to a friendly city or edge existed both then and at the end phase; rail in an enemy ZOC converts only in a friendly city (9.4).
- Second impulse ("blitz"): each player turn has two move-and-fight cycles. In the second, only second-impulse-capable units move, with no rail, no air and no replacements except HQs; units used in a first-impulse AV may not enter an enemy ZOC or attack (16.2). This gives a breakthrough-and-exploit cycle in place of advance after combat, which does not exist.
- Retreats: the attacker routes all retreats, may use a two-hex retreat that ends one hex away, and may thus "retreat forward" with his own units (13.4).
- Sea (10.0): one move per side per turn in each area (Baltic; Black Sea/Azov); none in the Caspian. Transfer, evacuation (+1) or invasion (two per side per game, Black Sea only, Axis not before July 1941). Survival: Black Sea 1-3, Baltic 1-2, -1 per friendly port in that sea; Russian Baltic moves +2 and need Leningrad. No land or rail move the same impulse.
- Air: air units are placed on a target within 8 hexes of a friendly HQ (15.2). Russian paratroops drop in snow first impulses within 8 hexes of Stavka, not into enemy ZOC, woods or mountains, once each (18.0).

## 5. Combat
- Land: units starting a combat phase in an enemy ZOC must attack, and every enemy whose ZOC they are in must be attacked (12.1-12.3); each unit fights once per impulse. Odds CRT, one d6, ratios rounded for the defender; attacks below 1-6 are illegal, and units forced into one surrender (12.5); soak-offs are allowed.
- CRT columns: 1-6/1-5, 1-4/1-3, 1-2, 1-1, 2-1, 3-1, 4-1, 5-1/6-1, 7-1 to 9-1. Results (13.3): AE, A1, AR, C, EX (attacker loses one unit first, then defender), DR, D1, DE, DS. At 1-1 rolls 1-6 give A1, A1, AR, C, EX, DR; at 4-1, C, EX, DR, D1, DE, DS.
- Modifiers: defender doubled for mountain, major city, river or strait (14.1); air and artillery shift columns.
- Automatic Victory (16.0): 10-1 odds during movement remove the defenders as surrendered; others may pass through. Russians may not AV before Nov/Dec 1942.
- Air: Stukas +3 columns, one per battle and one per HQ (1941 clear 3, light mud 1, mud 1; none in snow or after 1943). Sturmoviks +1 each, stackable, from Jul/Aug 1943 (3 in clear 1945); total shift with artillery capped at 3 (15.5-15.10). First impulse only, attacker only. No air combat or interdiction.
- Sea: naval combat not modeled.

## 6. Supply
- General (17.2): checked in the owner's end phase; trace at most 8 hexes (4 in snow) to a friendly city or to a friendly rail hex linked to a friendly city or edge, not through enemy ZOC or enemy cities, and not through partisans. Unsupplied units are eliminated, except paratroops, partisans and that turn's invaders. There is no gradual attrition.
- Combat (17.3): Russians and Finns always have it. Other Axis units in Russia, in snow turns of the first two winters, need to be in or next to an Axis city (looser in the second winter) or fight at half strength.
- Rail conversion is what extends Axis supply east. No naval supply beyond invasion; no air supply.

## 7. Other systems
- Sequence (4.0): weather; Axis first impulse movement and combat; Axis second impulse movement and combat; Axis end phase (railheads, supply); the same for the Russians, whose end phase also handles minor-ally surrender and partisans; sudden-death check after Jan/Feb.
- Weather (4.1.1): summer always clear, Jan/Feb always snow; Mar/Apr, Sep/Oct and Nov/Dec rolled on a d6 plus a cumulative DRM, each result adjusting the DRM (e.g., Nov/Dec 0-2 light mud +4, 3 mud -2, 4+ snow -3). Weather governs movement, Axis rail, supply range, air, Axis combat supply and paradrops.
- Command: only HQ ranges. Losing Hitler or Stalin freezes that nation's next impulse (11.3).
- Reinforcements (20.0) by OB schedule, with the Off-Map Units Box as reserve, triggered garrisons and fixed Axis withdrawals. Units exited off an enemy edge after 1942 must be matched by withdrawals or the opponent loses (20.10).
- Replacements: Axis only in May/June 1942-1944, by a fixed list tied to oil fields (21.4). Russians every turn by worker values plus an Archangel d6; workers double from May/June 1943 (22.0).
- Partisans hinder Axis rail and supply (19.0). Minor allies surrender on loss of capitals; Italy in Sep/Oct 1943 (24.0). No fog of war.

## 8. Notable observations for a rulebook-outline writer
- A compact numbered core with an illustrated example beside each hard rule is easy to learn and to cite; rail conversion alone has three examples.
- Essential data live elsewhere (MAC on player aids, CRT and TEC on the map, schedules on OB cards), so second-impulse allowances never appear in the book. Reproduce every chart the rules depend on.
- Some rules are misplaced: the Mud second-impulse limit appears only in a sea example, oil-field control only under Axis replacements. A single control rule for cities, junctions and oil fields would help.
- The developer calls rail conversion the most complex and disputed rule; its two-time test needs a step-by-step procedure and a reminder marker.
- The reformatted text introduces small errors (Russian end phase converting rail "to the Axis gauge"), and fan aids disagree with 12.5 (AE versus surrender) and with the map on Archangel DRMs; any reformatting needs a rule-by-rule check.
