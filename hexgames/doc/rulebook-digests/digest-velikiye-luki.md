# Velikiye Luki, Stalingrad of the North (Legion Wargames, 2023) -- component digest

Source files read, all in `C:\Library\War-Games\Velikiye-Luki, Stalingrad of the North\`: `Velikiye-Luki map.jpg` (the whole map, with turn track, loss track and CRT); `Velikiye Luki TEC scanned.jpg` (terrain key, terrain effects chart, counter guide, unit types and victory points); `VL counters front.jpg` and `VL counters back.jpg`. The folder holds no rulebook, so this digest covers only what the components show. Sections 4, 6 and 7 are therefore mostly empty.

`VLDNotes.pdf` in the same folder is the designer's notes for a different game: Dirk Blennemann's *Velikye Luki* (Moments in History, 2000), built on his "T3" Operational Warfare System. Those notes are summarized at the end because they state design purposes clearly, but none of their mechanics apply to the Legion game.

## 0. Rulebook structure
Not available.

## 1. Situation and design
- Situation: historical. The Soviet offensive against the German-held rail town of Velikiye Luki, November 1942 to February 1943, and the German relief attempts.
- Design and graphics: game design Michael Taylor; map graphics Randy Lein; counters © 2022, map and charts © 2023.
- Scale: 10 game turns of 8-9 days each (Nov. 19-27 to Feb. 8-16), on the turn track. The map is about 10 hex columns (01xx-09xx) by 10 rows (xx00-xx09). The hex size is not printed. Units are battalions, regiments, brigades and divisions.
- Scenarios: unknown; the turn track suggests one 10-turn game.
- Victory points (TEC sheet), German and Russian values: Velikiye Luki 9 and 1; Novosokolniki 3 and 2; each other single objective hex 1 and 1; each Russian step lost 1 VP to the German; each German unit lost 1 VP to the Russian. Objective hexes are marked on the map with a red star, and a German cross where the Germans start in control.
- Design features visible on the components: a garrison that may refuse retreat (German units defending in Velikiye Luki may ignore a retreat by taking one more step loss); fortification markers worth one or two CRT columns; fractional movement costs that distinguish ski and mountain troops from motorized and tank units.

## 2. Map
- Hex terrain (TEC): Open costs 1.5 MP, no combat effect. Wooded costs 2 MP, but 1.5 MP for ski and mountain units and 3 MP for tank, mechanized and motorized units, and shifts one column left. Hill uses the other terrain in the hex and shifts one column left. Town (Velikiye Luki and Novosokolniki only) uses the other terrain and shifts one column left, plus fortification for German units in Velikiye Luki. Village and lakes use the other terrain in the hex for both movement and combat.
- Hexside features: Major river hexside +1 MP and one column left. Minor river hexside no movement cost and a -1 die roll modifier. Bridge hexside negates the river for movement but not for combat. Prohibited hexside: tanks may not cross; other units treat it as the other terrain in the hex.
- Center-to-center features: road and railroad, 1 MP, combat effect from the other terrain in the hex. The Lovat river runs north to south through the middle of the map past Velikiye Luki; railroads converge on Velikiye Luki and Novosokolniki.
- Other features: a column of German entry hexes on the west edge and of Russian entry hexes on the east edge. The Russian edge is divided by arriving formation (2nd MC, 5th GSC, 3rd SA). Named towns and villages. Turn track with reinforcement symbols (infantry or mechanized, German cross or Soviet star) on turns 3-7. Loss track 1-15. German and Russian eliminated-unit boxes. The CRT is printed on the map.
- Markers: Game Turn; Victory Points (x1 and +10 on the back) for each side; Fortifications -1L and -2L.

## 3. Units
- Kinds (unit-type key): infantry, mechanized infantry, mountain infantry, ski infantry, motorized, tank, artillery, engineers, anti-aircraft, security. Infantry, mountain, ski, engineer and security units may be replaced with an infantry replacement point. Colors: Russian (tan), Russian Guards (red), German (gray), Luftwaffe (light blue), SS (black).
- Sizes: battalion (II) to division (XX); the counter guide shows echelon, turn of entry, unit type, unit name, a support-unit dot, combat strength and movement allowance.
- Counts (counted from the counter scans; approximate): about 31 Russian units, 4 of them Guards rifle divisions (9-6, 8-6, 7-6, 7-6), the rest rifle and mechanized brigades and divisions, tank brigades and ski brigades. About 28 German units, including one Luftwaffe field division (6-6) and one SS brigade (6-8), plus regiments, Kampfgruppen, security battalions, an engineer battalion, an anti-aircraft unit and an artillery command (ARKO).
- Counter values: two factors, combat strength and movement allowance (e.g. 4-8, 7-6). Movement allowances are 6 or 8.
- Steps: about 17 Russian and 13 German units have a reduced back (e.g. 7-6 to 3-6, 9-6 to 4-6, 5-8 to 1-8); the rest have a blank back and so have a single step.

## 5. Combat
- Land: odds CRT with columns 1:3, 1:2, 1:1, 2:1, 3:1, 4:1, 5:1 and rows 0-6 for one die with modifiers (the minor-river -1 creates row 0). Results: NE no effect; A1, A2 attacker loses one or two steps; EX both sides lose one step; EX1 both lose one step and the defender retreats one hex; DR defender retreats one hex; D1 defender loses one step; D1R loses one step and retreats one hex; D2R loses two steps and retreats two hexes; D3R loses three steps and retreats two hexes. Terrain and fortifications act as column shifts to the left, so the order of modifiers does not arise.
- Sea and air: not modeled on the components.

## 8. Notable observations for a rulebook-outline writer
- Combat effects expressed only as column shifts and one die modifier make the terrain chart short and the modifier order irrelevant.
- The TEC sheet puts the terrain key, terrain chart, counter guide, unit-type key and victory points on one page, a compact model for a player aid.
- Fractional movement costs (1.5 MP) let one chart separate ski, foot and mechanized movement without a separate table.

## Appendix: Blennemann's notes on the T3 system (a different game)
Blennemann's *Velikye Luki* (MiH 2000) uses version 2.0 of the system from *Turning the Tables* and *Drive to the Baltic!*. His notes state the purpose of each system:
- "Mechanical simplicity": structure the many factors so that the player is not loaded with memorization or bookkeeping; this is not the same as a simple game.
- Sequence of play: each turn is split into three segments per side, and in each segment the player chooses whether to fight or move first. This gives some interaction without a fully interactive system, and lets players imitate "assault, then breakthrough" or "maneuver, then combat".
- C3I points: an abstract measure of command efficiency and resources whose availability is uncertain, which prevents perfectly synchronized operations.
- Combat: tactical superiority and drawn combat chits fold air, artillery and doctrine into the combat roll, so they need no separate rules and are kept out of the player's control.
- A Kessel (pocket) track abstracts supply dumps, isolation, attrition, air supply and release.
- Activity points replace separate movement and combat points.
- The 7th Flieger-Division is left out as an optional reinforcement because the other side's response would be pure speculation.
