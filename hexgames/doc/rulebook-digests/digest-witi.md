# War in the Ice (SPI 1978) -- rulebook digest

Source files read: witi.txt (Rules of Play and Situation Briefing, v1.0, complete); witi-charts.txt (chart sheet); War_in_the_Ice_map.jpg (low-resolution map image, read for the charts printed on the map: 6.3, 6.4, 10.3, 11.6, 15.5, and the Supply Tracks); the counter sheet in the scanned "rules counters map.pdf" (counter layout only).

## 0. Rulebook structure
1.0 Introduction; 2.0 General Course of Play; 3.0 Game Equipment (Map, Pieces, Charts, Glossary, Inventory, Scale); 4.0 Victory (RP Awards and Losses, Tracking, Levels); 5.0 Sequence of Play; 6.0 Land Movement; 7.0 Air Movement (When, How, Escort, Interception, Basing, Air Ferry); 8.0 Initiative and Stacking; 9.0 Combat (Air, Anti-Aircraft, Land, Breakoff, tables 9.5-9.9); 10.0 Resources and Unit Purchasing (Paying, Arrival, Cost Chart); 11.0 Supply (Unsupplied, Airdrop, Air Transport, Capacity, Capture/Demolition/Transfer, Cost Chart); 12.0 Detection (Air, Land Movement, Sensor, Type Concealment, Evading, Voluntary, Tables); 13.0 Satellites (Recon Launch, Killer Satellites, Lasers vs. Satellites); 14.0 Special Unit Capabilities (Engineers, Hovertanks, Paratroops/Airmobile, Lasers, Garrisons, Droids); 15.0 Weather (Good, Normal, Poor, Winter, Table); 16.0 Optional Rules (Magnetic Disruption, Sea Transport, Naval Blockade, Sensor-Activated Mines, Aerial Sensor Emplacement, Political Influence); 17.0 Scenarios (17.1-17.4); 18.0 The Science Fiction Game; 19.0 Subterran Special Unit Capabilities (Lifters, Ice Drills, Converters, Psimasters); 20.0 Designer's Notes.

The rules use SPI case numbering (General Rule, Procedure, Cases). Movement and combat come first, while supply and detection, which govern the early phases of each turn, come later. Charts are split between the map (terrain, movement, costs, weather) and a separate sheet (combat, detection, Subterran tables). A long fictional "Situation Briefing" follows the rules: a campaign history of 1991-92, a 2126 archaeology article on the Subterrans, and background essays on the war's origins, land forces, air war, and Antarctica.

## 1. Situation and design
- **Situation:** A hypothetical war in Antarctica in the 1990s among the USA, USSR, and a "South American Union" (SAU) (1.0). The future is established by scenario introductions and the briefing. Energy surveys (oil, coal, gas, uranium) in the late 1980s lead to armed survey teams, a 1987 firefight at Wilkes, a secret skirmish war, and open war from 4 November 1991. Invented hardware fills the setting: articulated "snowtanks," remotely operated droid tanks, Soviet hovertanks, laser anti-air batteries, AWACS-type EW aircraft, and recon and killer satellites. Victory levels are described as political consequences, up to the risk of nuclear escalation (4.33). An optional version adds the "Subterrans," an ancient army sleeping in stasis beneath the ice (18.0).
- **Scale:** About 130 km per hex; 15 days per turn (November I to October II, 24 boxes); units of several hundred men and dozens of vehicles, or squadrons of 12-20 aircraft (3.6). Two or three players, plus an optional Subterran player (18.21).
- **Scenarios (17.0):** 17.1 USA vs USSR, Gradual Escalation: 12 turns from November I; each side buys at most 100 RP of land units, 100 RP of air units, and 5 RP of supply; no unit purchases on the first two turns. 17.2 adds the SAU as a third player. 17.3 Prior Buildup: 24 turns; 250/200/15 RP; up to three temporary bases at start; 50 RP penalty for entering an SAU base hex. 17.4 is the three-player Prior Buildup (SAU 200/160/15). Victory is the net Resource Point (RP) difference at game end: +500 Decisive, +300 Substantive, +100 Marginal, 0-99 Draw. RP are spent on purchases, lost when units die, and gained for capturing enemy bases (100 primary, 60 secondary coastal, 40 secondary inland, nothing for temporary bases) (4.1).
- **Usual course:** The rules say nothing on balance or typical play. The fictional history shows a six-month war decided by aircraft with superior EW, with both sides trading bases in "Bloody March" and a ceasefire when the April gales arrive. Its lessons work as play hints: stay within air cover, keep garrisons and air-landed reserves, deploy sensors, and keep lasers in bases.
- **Variants:** Early Victory, Open-Ended (costs escalate in later turn blocks), Winter Start, True Alliance (one player runs two nations with a 300 RP handicap), More Active SAU. Optional rules are listed in section 0.
- **Key design features:** (a) Resource Points serve as both budget and score, so every purchase and loss counts against victory; surrender costs only 20% of a unit's value because lives matter more than equipment in a limited, economic war (10.13, 20.0). (b) Units cannot be attacked until detected; the designer likens play to a WWII Pacific carrier battle (12.0, 20.0). (c) Supply is tracked point by point because the designer judged Antarctic logistics too important to abstract (20.0). (d) EW ratings enter nearly every combat and detection table. (e) Nuclear weapons are excluded on principle (20.0).

## 2. Map
- **Terrain (6.3, from map):** Movement cost for armor/infantry/hovertank: snow 1/1/1; shelf ice 5 (4 in winter)/3/1; mountain/glacier 4/1/prohibited; snow and shelf ice ("mixed") 2/1/1; mountain/glacier and shelf ice 4/3/prohibited. Terrain has no effect on land combat (9.0), but it modifies detection rolls (12.7) and breakoff (+2 shelf ice, -2 mountain/glacier, 9.9), gives hovertanks +3 anti-armor on all-shelf-ice hexes (14.21), and doubles temporary-base cost in mountain/glacier (14.12).
- **Linear features:** None: no rivers, roads, or railroads. Land units may not enter all-sea hexes or cross all-sea hexsides (6.22).
- **Other features:** Printed primary and secondary bases for each nation (by my approximate count from the low-resolution image, about eight or nine each, for example McMurdo, Pole, and Wilkes for the USA; Molodezhnaya, Vostok, and Mirnyy for the USSR). Also coastline (any partial-sea hex is coastal, 10.24), stasis complexes, and the South Magnetic Pole (hex 2233, 16.1). Displays: Game-Turn Track with a winter band, a two-box Transit Track, Supply and Victory Tracks for each nation.
- **Markers:** Supply markers for bases, vans, and transports; Detected, Unsupplied, Melted, Orbit Reference, temporary base, sensor, Tactical Option chits, and multiplier counters for the Victory Track.
- **Unique features:** Crevasses appear only as laser-melted hexes, which double movement cost and neutralize bases (14.4). Weather is continent-wide.

## 3. Units
- **Kinds:** Land units are mechanized task forces, droid teams, van convoys (supply carriers), lasers, garrisons, engineers, airmobile teams (USA), paratroops (USSR, SAU), and hovertanks (USSR only). Air units are fighters, ground attack/spotters (GS), EW aircraft, light and heavy transports, transport helicopters, and attack helicopters. The USA and USSR also have STAR and MARS recon satellites and KISS killer satellites. Installations are temporary bases and sensors. Subterran units are sleeper infantry, lifters, ice drills, energy converters, and psimasters.
- **Counts (17.1, 17.2):** USA: 35 land units (8 mech, 9 droid, 6 van, 3 laser, 2 garrison, 3 engineer, 4 airmobile), 35 air units, and 4 satellites. USSR: 35 land units (7 mech, 8 droid, 6 van, 3 laser, 3 hovertank, 2 garrison, 2 engineer, 4 para), 30 air units, and 4 satellites. SAU: 30 land units and 26 air units, with no helicopters or satellites. Subterrans have at most 16 sleepers, 4 lifters, 2 drills, 2 converters, and 2 psimasters, depending on the option (18.3). There are 400 counters in all (3.5). The counter mix limits purchases, and eliminated units return to the pool.
- **Counter layout:** Land units show a name, a type symbol, and four factors: anti-armor, anti-infantry, anti-air, and EW rating (US mech 7-2-3-5; a laser shows "L" for anti-air). Air units also show four factors (attack helicopter 9-3-3-2). These appear to be anti-ground, air combat, range, and EW, but the rules never state the order. Movement allowance is by type on chart 6.4. Garrisons have a base strength set and a field strength set (14.52). All land units except garrisons are back-printed with a generic armor or infantry symbol (12.41).
- **Steps:** None. Units are eliminated whole.

## 4. Movement
- **Land (6.0):** Allowances by weather, good/normal/poor (6.4, from map): US/SAU mech, droid, and van 6/4/2; laser 5/3/2; garrison 3/3/1; engineer 4/3/2; airmobile and para 1/1/0. USSR mech, droid, and van 6/4/3; hovertank 8/2/0; para 2/2/1. Players alternate moving stacks in Initiative order, set by a two-dice roll; the Soviet player always goes first in poor weather (8.2). There are no zones of control (6.29), and stacking is unlimited (8.41). A detected unit stops on entering a hex with detected enemy units (6.23). Entering a hex with undetected enemies triggers a detection roll (6.24). Detected opponents in the same hex cannot leave, except hovertanks and lifters (14.22, 19.14). Unsupplied units cannot move (6.28). There is no strategic movement, retreat, or advance after combat.
- **Sea:** No naval units. New units reach coastal bases through the Transit Track, taking two turns for US and USSR units and one for SAU units (10.21). A player with no coastal base may make an "amphibious landing" at least five hexes from the enemy for 100 RP (10.24). Optional sea transport shifts up to 8/8/5 units between coastal bases per summer turn, 6/5/4 in winter (16.2). Optional blockade closes one enemy coastal base for a phase, once per game, for 50 RP (16.3).
- **Air (7.0):** Air units are placed in any hex within range of their base, without tracing a path, and return to the same base. Each type flies only in certain phases (7.1): transports in Airdrop and Air Transport; EW and GS in Air Detection; fighters, GS, EW, attack helicopters, and troop-carrying transports in the simultaneous Scramble Phase. Fighters escort only if they start stacked with the escorted unit (7.3), and they intercept only over the mission hex (7.4). Air Ferry moves any air unit between friendly bases regardless of range, after which the unit does nothing else that turn (7.6). Paratroops and airmobile troops ride transports at 6 SP of capacity each and drop into battle hexes during Scramble. In normal weather paratroops cannot drop and only helicopters may insert airmobile troops; in poor weather no drops are allowed (14.3, 15.23, 15.33).

## 5. Combat
- **Land (9.3):** Land combat occurs only in hexes where both sides are detected, and there it is mandatory (9.36). Each side uses anti-armor or anti-infantry strength according to the enemy's majority type, adds the anti-ground strength of its scrambled aircraft, and the difference is the combat differential. The larger side is called the attacker. Both sides then secretly choose Tactical Option chits. Attacker choices are Cavalry Charge, Methodical Advance, Air-Intensive, and Combined Arms. Defender choices are Retreat Leaving Screen, Hedgehog, Mobile Defense, and Surrender. Some chits require a certain force mix. The cross-index gives a dice modifier of -3 to +3 (9.33-9.35). Two dice on the Land CRT (columns 0 to 8+) give attacker and defender units lost, chosen by their owners; aircraft are never lost in this segment (9.37). Rounds repeat until both sides agree to stop, a breakoff roll succeeds (9.9, modified by EW, weather, terrain, and force mix), or one side is destroyed. Surrender eliminates the defender's land units at 20% of their RP cost (10.13).
- **Sea:** Not modeled.
- **Air (9.1, 9.2):** In air-to-air combat, the difference in air combat strength gives each side a number of "lock-ons," which are rolls on the Hit Table. The column used is net EW, a side's highest EW rating minus the enemy's highest. Each hit eliminates one aircraft. Anti-aircraft fire comes next: each detected land unit rolls on the 9.7 table for a hit or an abort, and ordinary units may fire only at helicopters, GS, and fighters. Lasers fire at any aircraft, ignore EW, never cause aborts, and are stronger in a friendly base (9.24-9.25). Ground support adds the anti-ground strength of aircraft to a land battle. Aircraft cannot attack land units unless friendly land units are present, and there is no interdiction.
- **Satellites (13.0):** A recon satellite makes three straight passes per turn through an orbit reference hex and detects as an EW-9 air unit. STAR satellites repeat their path; MARS satellites may shift theirs up to five hexes. Each satellite may malfunction each turn (2-4 for US, 2-6 for Soviet). Enemy KISS killer satellites or lasers may attack it.

## 6. Supply
- **Land (11.0):** Supply Points (SP) cost 1 RP per 15 SP and appear at once in coastal bases (10.21). They are held in bases (primary 100, secondary 50, temporary 25), vans (20), and transports (heavy 30, light 12, helicopter 6) (3.4, Supply Track on map). In the Supply Phase, a unit is supplied only if SP are in its own hex. The cost per turn is 1 for van, airmobile, and light transport; 2 for mech, droid, para, engineer, and helicopter; and 3 for all others (11.6, from map). There are no supply lines to trace. Vans carry SP overland. Airdrop supplies units in the field at triple cost, or loads a van at one-third efficiency (11.2). Air transport moves SP between bases at no loss (11.3). Unsupplied land units cannot move, lose 3 from their combat strengths, and suffer +4 on anti-aircraft rolls; unsupplied aircraft may only ferry (11.12-11.13). Units die only after two consecutive poor-weather turns spent unsupplied outside a friendly base (15.38). Half of any captured SP is destroyed (11.52). Players may destroy their own SP to deny them (11.53).
- **Bases:** An engineer with a van carrying 5 SP (10 SP in mountain/glacier) builds a temporary base; each player may build up to four (14.1). Bases are never destroyed, only captured or neutralized. Units in any base hex are always detected (12.5).
- **Sea:** Abstracted. The Transit Track represents shipping time.
- **Air:** Airdrop and air transport are central to supply, and intercepting supply flights is the main air contest.

## 7. Other systems
- **Sequence (5.2):** 1 Purchase/Arrival; 2 Weather; 3 Supply; 4 Airdrop; 5 Air Transport; 6 Air Detection; 7 Satellite; 8 Land Movement; 9 Air Scramble; 10 Combat (each battle runs Air-to-Air, Anti-Aircraft, Paradrop Insertion, Tactical Decision, Resolution, Breakoff, Basing); 11 Construction; 12 Air Ferry; 13 Turn Advance.
- **Command:** Not modeled beyond die-rolled Initiative and alternating moves.
- **Purchases (10.3, from map; costs USA/USSR/SAU):** van, para, and light transport 5; airmobile and transport helicopter 8; mech, heavy transport, and attack helicopter 10/12/10; droid, GS, and fighter 12/12/14; engineer and hovertank 15/13/13; garrison and EW 25/25/28; laser 40/35/40; KISS 45/50; STAR 70/80; MARS 85/100. A droid's elimination costs only 2/4/5 RP. Deficit spending is allowed (10.11).
- **Weather (15.0):** One roll for the whole continent each turn. The sequence of play says "a die," but table 15.5 uses two-dice ranges. Good weather falls from 2-7 in GT 1-3 to only a 2 in GT 17-18, where poor weather is 5-12. Lasers may melt ice only in good weather. Normal weather slows movement, hampers land and sensor detection, and grounds paratroops. Poor weather slows movement further and grounds all aircraft except heavy transports, EW aircraft, and US fighters. It also clears all detection and type markers, removes melted hexes, and neutralizes temporary bases. Winter (GT 7-22) only lowers the armor cost of shelf ice to 4 (15.4).
- **Fog of war:** Units are hidden until detected by air EW or GS flights, land contact, sensors (2 MP to place, eight per player), or satellites (12.0, 13.0). A second roll reveals unit type (12.4). Satellite costs may be paid late to hide which type was launched (13.17).
- **Special rules:** The three-player rules allow open negotiation, alliances made hex by hex, broken deals, bribes in RP, and shared supply that loses half in transfer (17.2). Political Influence results range from half-price reinforcements to forced withdrawal (16.6). Subterrans revive from stasis complexes, are supplied within two hexes of a complex, and win if the human nations end in a Draw (18-19).

## 8. Notable observations for a rulebook-outline writer
- The annotated sequence of play (5.2) works as a complete play summary and is worth copying. The body, however, places supply and detection (phases 3-8) after combat (phase 10).
- Key values are split among the rules, the map, and the chart sheet. Supply capacities appear only on the Supply Track, and the counter factors are never explained in words. New rules should include a labeled counter diagram and keep all values in one place.
- The step-by-step worked examples (9.13, 9.27, 9.38) explain the multi-step procedures well.
- There are small slips: "a die" against a two-dice table, a misnumbered case (18.14 for 8.14), and wrong cross-references (Cases 10.12, 10.23). Cross-references should be checked systematically.
- The fictional campaign history makes the future setting credible and teaches strategy through the commanders' mistakes, without adding length to the rules themselves.
- A single currency for budget and score keeps the limited-war premise present in every decision.
