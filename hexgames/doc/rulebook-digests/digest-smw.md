# Stalin Moves West -- rulebook digest

Source files read: smw.txt (pdftotext of Stalin_Moves_West_Rules_V8F-ERULES.pdf; Decision Games, World at War 58, 2018; designer Joseph Miranda; 16 pages). The Terrain Effects Chart, both Combat Results Tables and their result definitions, and the Weather, Air Superiority, Air Landing and German Reorganization charts are printed on the map, not in the rules text, so their contents are not given here.

## 0. Rulebook structure
1.0 Introduction; 2.0 Components (map, counters, unit sizes, abbreviations, markers, charts, scale); 3.0 Set Up; 4.0 Victory Conditions; 5.0 Sequence of Play; 6.0 Map Geography and Hex Control; 7.0 Mobilization Points (MOP); 8.0 Mobilization and Reinforcements; 9.0 Reorganization; 10.0 Movement Phase; 11.0 Exploit Movement; 12.0 Stacking; 13.0 Zones of Control; 14.0 Combat; 15.0 Retreat after Combat; 16.0 Pursuit; 17.0 Supply; 18.0 Soviet Untried Units; 19.0 Headquarters; 20.0 Airpower; 21.0 Unique Units (fortresses, Berlin garrison, mountain, partisans, airborne, Fliegerkorps VIII); 22.0 Weather; 23.0 Fog of War; 24.0 Reserve Areas; 25.0 Scenarios (25.1 No Barbarossa, 25.2 Pre-emptive War, 25.3 Variant: No Stalinist Purge); 26.0 Optional Rules (air missions, flak, defensive supply, ground and airbase attrition, naval operations). Two Mobilization Charts close the book.

The book roughly follows the turn: victory and sequence first, then the MOP economy, movement, combat and supply, then special systems, scenarios and options. Cases are decimal numbered; there is no basic/advanced split. Red marks errata and blue marks examples. There are a few short examples and one labeled designer's note (13.7). The scenarios are in the rulebook, and the main charts are on the map.

## 1. Situation and design
- **Situation:** a hypothetical 1941-42 war in which Germany does not invade the USSR and Stalin attacks westward instead (1.0). The premise is set out in two short scenario paragraphs, not a designer's essay. In No Barbarossa, Germany turns against Britain in the West and the Mediterranean, and the Soviets take the opening (25.1). In Pre-emptive War, Hitler is massing in the East for a possible invasion, and Stalin strikes first (25.2). The rules give no further justification, sources or historiography. Any such discussion would be in the magazine article, which was not read.
- **Scale:** about 70 km per hex; one month per turn in summer and two in winter; mostly armies and corps, with army-group HQs; two players; one d6; 176 counters (2.0, 2.10).
- **Scenarios:** two, each seven turns (3.2), plus one variant usable with either. No calendar start date is printed.
- **Victory (4.0):** A side wins at once if it controls every mobilization and resource hex. Otherwise the Soviets score 10 for Berlin, 4 per Axis resource hex, 2 per Axis mobilization hex and 1 per other city west of the border. The Axis scores 4 per Soviet mobilization hex and 1 per other city east of the border. Soviet minus Axis gives the result: 20+ Soviet Decisive, 15-19 Operational, 10-14 Marginal, 5-9 Draw, 0-4 Axis Operational, below 0 Axis Decisive. A note says the Soviets must attack to win.
- **Course of play:** the rules say nothing beyond that note.
- **Variations:** No Stalinist Purge (25.3) and the optional rules (26.0).
- **Key design features:**
  - *Two attacker-chosen CRTs* (14.12). Assault is always allowed. Mobile needs a mechanized unit and is barred against cities.
  - *Strong ZOCs* (13.0). Per the designer's note (13.7), they represent Eastern Front ferocity and sub-scale action, and they replace a concentric-attack bonus. Retreating into an EZOC is fatal even into a friendly-occupied hex.
  - *Supply as a spendable multiplier* (17.0). Units always fight and move at printed values; supply units are spent to boost them.
  - *Point-buy mobilization* (7.0). Income depends on hexes held, so the economy shifts as ground changes hands.
- **Surprise and unpreparedness:**
  - *Axis caught off guard.* In No Barbarossa, a turn-1 "Shock Effect" bars the Axis from earning MOP, mobilizing, or combining corps into armies. The Axis starts with 0 MOP against the Soviet 9+1d6. In Pre-emptive War, the Axis is partly ready: it starts with 1d6 MOP against the Soviet 12+1d6, earns MOP on turn 1 but cannot mobilize, may form armies at setup, and deploys more units. In both, the Axis turn-1 MOP bonus is 0 (7.2), off-map armies become available only on turn 2 (9.4), and the Soviets move first each turn (5.1).
  - *Red Army weakened by the purges.* Soviet units are drawn at random and placed face down, and their strength shows only in combat (18.0). Soviet supply units may prove x1 or x2. The Tukhachevsky HQ is set aside. Tank armies, and in No Barbarossa also shock armies, cannot be built until the first mud or snow turn. Further handicaps: lower stacking, exploitation only within HQ radius (11.0), one airbase per attack against two German (20.2), and airborne losses on 1-2 against the Germans' 1 (21.5).
  - The No Purge variant reveals Soviet units when drawn and makes Tukhachevsky buildable. It allows tank and shock armies from turn 1, permits an army plus a mechanized corps per hex, and frees mechanized exploitation from HQ radius. In compensation the Axis gains 1d6 MOP.

## 2. Map
- **Terrain types:** Costs are on the map's TEC. The text mentions clear (1 MP), forest (2 MP in mud, so 1 MP otherwise; 22.2), rough (mountain attack bonus), marsh (supply may enter but not pass through), city (no Mobile CRT), all-sea and prohibited terrain. Terrain defense is a doubling of the defender, never cumulative and never more than x2; attacker terrain is ignored (14.11).
- **Hexside features:** River and lake hexsides double the defender only if all attackers cross them, and not on top of another doubling; winter freezes them (14.11, 22.3). The USSR border bounds both rail nets.
- **Roads and railroads:** Railroads carry roads (10.6). Road movement costs 1/2 MP per hex, ignores terrain, and works regardless of supply or ownership, but not in the USSR in mud or winter.
- **Other features:** mobilization and resource hexes (locations on the map only), Berlin, ports and coastal hexes on the Baltic and Black Seas, and off-map reserve areas reached from friendly map edges: STAVKA for the Soviets, OKW for the Axis (10.9). The map also carries the turn and MOP tracks. There are no neutrals (6.0). Control is by occupation, by last passage, or by start-line ownership, and an EZOC cancels control of an empty hex (6.3).
- **Markers:** two turn markers, two MOP markers and one weather marker (2.8); there are no supply or control markers.

## 3. Units
- **Kinds:** Mechanized units are tank/panzer, mechanized/panzergrenadier, flak and Axis-allied mobile. Non-mechanized units are infantry, combined arms, shock, mountain, amphibious, cavalry, airborne, fortress, partisan and security. Support units are HQs, supply units and airbases (Soviet air armies, German air corps). Sizes run from army-group HQ through army and Abteilung to corps (2.5). The Axis side comprises Germany, Hungary, Romania, Slovakia, Italy and volunteers.
- **Counts:** The rules give no split. Estimated from the setups: in Pre-emptive War, the Soviets start with about 57 units (13 combined arms armies, 7 infantry armies, 9 mechanized corps, 6 supply, 5 air armies, 6 fortifications, and others) and the Axis with about 50 (about 44 German, 6 allied). No Barbarossa has about 48 Soviet and 37 Axis units. The rest are reinforcements.
- **Counter layout:** Combat units show attack-defense-movement. Support units show a radius or range at upper left and a support factor or multiplier at lower left, plus defense and MA. An asterisk marks German corps eligible to form armies.
- **Flip sides:** Axis backs carry a flag. The Berlin garrison is a 0-4-0 fortress that may flip to a panzergrenadier corps once Soviets come within four hexes (21.2). Soviet units except HQs have an untried back. There is no step reduction; German armies break down into corps to absorb losses (9.1).

## 4. Movement
- **Land:** Units pay MP per the TEC, with a minimum move of one hex (10.5). Stacking limits are one army or three corps for the Soviets and one army or four corps (or an army plus a corps) for the Axis, plus one support unit and one fortress. Limits are checked at the end of each movement and combat phase; excess units are eliminated, and nationalities may not mix (12.0). All units exert ZOC at all times. A unit entering an EZOC must stop, and it may leave only at the start of a move into a hex free of EZOC (13.3, 13.4).
- **Rail (10.7):** Movement Phase only. It costs half the printed MA and requires a start in supply. Range is unlimited on the own net, which for the Soviets lies east of the USSR border only and for the Axis west of it only. The cap is 3 army equivalents per phase (an army, 3 corps, or a support unit).
- **Forced march (10.8):** Expending a supply unit raises the MA of all units in its radius: doubled for the Axis, times the revealed multiplier (possibly x1) for the Soviets.
- **Exploit (11.0):** No rail. Eligible are German panzer, panzergrenadier and HQ units, and Soviet armor, mechanized and HQ units within HQ radius.
- **Reserve areas (24.0):** Entering costs +1 MP and ends movement for the turn.
- **Sea:** optional only (26.6). Each side gets one amphibious move per turn, Soviets in the Black Sea and Axis in the Baltic. The move must start or end in a port, expends a supply unit, and requires an Air Landing Table roll. The Axis alone has one automatic port-to-port transfer per turn.
- **Air:** Airborne units start with an airbase, drop up to its range, and are lost on a 1-2 for Soviets or a 1 for Germans. The Soviets drop only in the Movement Phase, the Axis in either phase (21.5). Optional air transfer has no loss roll (26.1).

## 5. Combat
- **Land:** Combat is voluntary between adjacent units. Each unit and each hex is involved in combat once per phase, and nationalities may not combine unless a German HQ is in range (19.3). The exploit combat phase is open to Soviet mechanized units in HQ radius, all German mechanized units, and other German units in HQ radius (14.1). The odds are attack/defense x 100, read on percentage columns. One d6 is rolled on the Assault or Mobile CRT. Result codes named in the text are DA, DL1, BL1 and DR.
- **Bonuses (14.8):** one HQ's support factor; attack supply (spend a supply unit to multiply attackers in its radius, Axis x2, Soviet revealed on use); airbase support. All may combine.
- **Losses and retreat:** The owner chooses losses, and armies may break down to lose a corps (14.15). A retreat may not enter an EZOC or an enemy hex; a unit unable to retreat is eliminated, and an army entering an EZOC loses a corps per such hex (15.1).
- **Pursuit (16.0):** Usually 1 hex for non-mechanized units and more for mechanized. EZOC is ignored in the first hex.
- **Special units:** Mountain units double their attack into rough, triple with supply. Fortresses are static.
- **Sea:** not modeled.
- **Air (20.0):** Tactical support adds airbase factors, and supply can double them. Air superiority attacks enemy airbases on the Air Superiority Table before ground combat. Optional interdiction lets German airbases strike Soviet supply units. Winter halves air support.

## 6. Supply
- **Land:** A unit is in general supply within a supply unit's hex radius, or on or adjacent to a railroad connected to one. The radius may not pass through enemy units, all-sea or marsh hexes, or out of an EZOC (17.2, 17.4). Supply units, fortresses, partisans and reserve-area units are always supplied, and general supply never uses up the supply unit. Being out of supply only blocks rail movement, army building and fortress placement, plus optional winter attrition (a 6 in a city, a 5-6 elsewhere; 26.4). Mud shrinks the radius in the USSR to one hex (22.2). Isolation is not modeled.
- **Sea:** abstracted; there is no shipping system.
- **Air:** no air supply. Airborne units out of supply roll for attrition (21.5).

## 7. Other systems
- **Sequence (5.1):** The Soviet turn runs Mobilization, Movement, Combat, Exploit, Exploit Combat. The Axis turn is the same with a Reorganization phase after Mobilization. A joint phase follows with optional attrition and turn advance.
- **Mobilization (7.0, 8.0):** Soviet income is 1 MOP per mobilization hex plus 3/4/4/5/5/6/6 by turn. Axis income is 1 per mobilization hex and 2 per resource hex plus 0/4/5/6/7/8/9. Captured hexes yield nothing, and the cap is 19. Units appear in own mobilization hexes or reserve areas. HQs cannot be replaced, and German armies are formed only from corps. Costs run 1-6 MOP (for example, Soviet combined arms army 3, shock army 4, HQ 5; German infantry corps 2, panzer corps 3, airborne 6); the grouping of some rows is inferred from the extracted layout.
- **Command:** Randomly drawn HQs add attack strength, enable Soviet exploitation and Axis multinational attacks, and are unaffected by the result.
- **Weather (22.0):** From turn 4 there is a die roll on the map's table. Mud reduces supply radius, raises forest to 2 MP and removes rail-roads in the USSR. Winter also freezes rivers, bans Baltic amphibious moves, and halves air support.
- **Fog of war (23.0):** Enemy map stacks may be examined; reserve boxes and untried units may not.

## 8. Notable observations for a rulebook-outline writer
- The hypothetical premise is handled in a few lines, with surprise expressed as economic and build restrictions ("Shock Effect"), not combat penalties. This is cheap to copy, but a designer's note explaining the calibration would help.
- Untried units with a "No Purge" switch make Soviet unreadiness both modeled and testable. A writer could use the same pattern for any premise that rests on unit quality.
- Supply as an optional multiplier keeps play light. Its few penalties are scattered (9.2, 10.7, 8.3, 26.4), however, and they should be gathered in one place.
- The terrain and combat tables live only on the map, so the rulebook cannot be read alone.
- Cross-references are unreliable. Airborne is cited as 21.7 (it is 21.5), untried units as 19.0 (they are 18.0), the 14.13 example divides by 13 instead of 12, and the text uses both "snow" and "winter." A writer should verify every reference.
