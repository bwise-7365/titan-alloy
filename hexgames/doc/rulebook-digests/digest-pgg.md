# Panzergruppe Guderian (SPI 1976) -- rulebook digest

Source files read: pgg.txt (Berger 2026 edition of the S&T rules with Avalon Hill additions; main source); pgg-v2.txt (OCR of the original SPI rules); PGG_CRT.pdf; Clarifications, Errata and Addenda; PGG-amendments.pdf (Beardsworth variant; "PGG II" variant by T. Alanthwaite); rule summary; PGG analysis.pdf (R. Simonsen, "Assaulting the Mystery"); countersheet images (front, back v2); PGG map, English.png.

## 0. Rulebook structure
1.0 Introduction; 2.0 How to Play the Game; 3.0 Game Equipment (3.1 Map, 3.2 Charts and Tables, 3.3 Playing Pieces, 3.4 How to Read the Units, 3.5 Parts Inventory); 4.0 Sequence of Play (4.1 Game-Turn, 4.2 Outline); 5.0 Initial Set-Up (5.1 Soviet Deployment, 5.2 Soviet First Turn Rules); 6.0 Movement (6.1 How to Move, 6.2 Inhibitions, 6.3 Rail, 6.4 Special Soviet Restrictions, 6.5 Overrun, 6.6 Disruption, 6.7 TEC); 7.0 Stacking (7.1 Soviet, 7.2 German, 7.3 Divisional Integration); 8.0 Zones of Control (8.1); 9.0 Combat (9.1 Which Units May Attack, 9.2 Multiple-Unit Combat, 9.3 Terrain, 9.4 Resolution, 9.5 CRT, 9.6 Results, 9.7 Retreats, 9.8 Advance); 10.0 Soviet Leaders (10.1 Rating, 10.2 Characteristics, 10.3 Capabilities, 10.4 Evacuation, optional); 11.0 Supply (11.1 German, 11.2 Soviet, 11.3 Effects); 12.0 Untried Units (12.1-12.4); 13.0 Interdiction (13.1-13.3 German Air, 13.4 Soviet); 14.0 Reinforcements (14.1 Provisional, 14.2 South-Western Front, 14.3 Scheduled); 15.0 Victory Conditions (15.1 Schedule, 15.2 Levels); 16.0 Reinforcement Schedules. Berger adds 17.0 Credits and 18.0 Charts.

The rules use the SPI case system (General Rule, Procedure, numbered Cases). A narrative walk-through of one turn (2.0) precedes the formal rules, which follow the turn order (movement, stacking, ZOC, combat) and then the special systems (Leaders, supply, untried units, interdiction, reinforcements). There is one scenario, few examples, and no designer's notes. The original prints the TEC, CRT, and turn track on the map.

## 1. Situation and design
- Situation: historical. The German panzer groups cross the Dnepr and drive on the communications junctions at Smolensk, July 1941, as a step toward Moscow (1.0).
- Scale: hex 10.5 km; turn 2 days (3-4 to 25-26 July); German regiments (mechanized) and divisions (infantry, cavalry); Soviet divisions and army Leaders; two players. Simonsen notes it suits solitaire play, since neither side knows Soviet strengths.
- Scenarios: one, 12 Game-Turns. The Soviets start with five armies deployed; the Germans start off-map and enter from the west (5.0). Victory is by net VP: the German player scores for cities and two east-edge hexes held at the end; the Soviet player scores for destroying whole German divisions and recapturing cities (15.0).
- Outcome: historically a German Marginal Victory; the rules say play is biased toward it, and optionally that result counts as a draw (15.2). Simonsen describes three stages: approach (Turns 1-4), assault on the Soviet main line near Smolensk and Roslavl (4-8), and breakout toward Vyazma, Kaluga, and 5915 (8-12); the winner is usually clear by Turn 10.
- Variations: Leader evacuation (10.4); Rail Cut markers (AH edition, 6.37); Beardsworth variant (1 VP to the Soviet per German step lost, 1 VP per minor city); PGG II (declining Smolensk value, VP for German step losses, bidding for sides, retreat priorities).
- Design features (no designer's notes; purposes inferred): untried Soviet units hide strength from both players and model the Red Army's uneven quality (12.0); Leaders tie Soviet supply and attack to army command (10.0); the German post-combat Mechanized Movement Phase gives blitzkrieg tempo (4.2); divisional integration rewards keeping panzer divisions together (7.3); Overrun and Disruption allow attack on the move (6.5-6.6); the first-turn die roll and the west-edge ban model Soviet surprise and confusion (5.2, 6.4).

## 2. Map
- Hex terrain (6.7): Clear 1 MP. Forest: non-motorized infantry 1 MP, others 2 MP; defender doubled. Swamp 2 MP; supply may be traced into, not through. Major City 1 MP, defender doubled. Minor City 1 MP, no effect.
- Hexsides and lines: River +2 MP German, +1 MP Soviet; defender doubled only if all attackers cross rivers (9.32). Lake hexside: no crossing, attack, retreat, or supply tracing; ZOC stops at all-lake hexsides (8.17). Road: infantry and cavalry 1 MP, others 1/2 MP; roads do not bridge rivers for movement, but do for German supply tracing (11.13). Railroad: cost of other terrain; used only for Soviet Rail Movement; railroads do bridge rivers. Terrain multiples are added, then one is subtracted (forest plus river = triple, 9.33).
- Other features: about 59 by 31 hexes, numbered xxyy, with hexrow 0100 at the west edge. VP cities: Vitebsk, Orsha, Mogilev, Smolensk (two hexes), Roslavl, Yelnya, Rzhev, Gzhatsk, Vyazma, Kaluga; VP hexes 5907 and 5915 are on the east edge. The map alone shows which cities are major. German supply source: road hex 0120 (west edge). Soviet supply source: the east edge. German entry areas A-H; Soviet entry hexes V, W, X, Z; Provisional entry hexes 1-6. An air interdiction limit runs along hexrow 40xx. The turn track's Soviet Turn 12 entry is wrong (16.1).
- Markers: three German Air Interdiction, one Soviet Interdiction, Disruption, Game-Turn; AH adds six Rail Cut and German Control markers. No out-of-supply markers.
- Special: no Soviet unit may enter hexrows 0100-0200 on Turns 1-6 (6.4).

## 3. Units
- Kinds: German panzer, panzergrenadier, and motorized regiments; Gross Deutschland and Lehr regiments; infantry and cavalry divisions. Soviet rifle, tank, and mechanized divisions (the last two count as "armored," 3.4) and army Leaders. Air power and partisans appear only as markers.
- Counts (counted from the countersheet image; approximate). German: 9 panzer divisions (one 4-10 panzer and two 2-10 panzergrenadier regiments each), 6 motorized divisions (two 3-10 regiments each; SS Das Reich has three), GD 4-10, Lehr 3-10, 18 infantry divisions at 9-7, and one 4-5 cavalry division: about 61 units. Soviet: 78 rifle divisions, 10 tank and 10 mechanized divisions, and 15 Leaders. The schedules call for only 11 armored divisions; the surplus is deliberate, to vary Soviet armor quality (3.4). Rifle arrivals (about 88 scheduled, 12 Provisional, up to 10 South-Western Front) exceed the 78 counters, so eliminated counters are recycled (12.4).
- Counters: German units show Combat Strength and Movement Allowance (9-7). Tried Soviet rifle units show Attack-Defense-Movement (3-5-6); Soviet armor shows Combat-Movement (5-10). Untried faces show "?" and MA. Leaders show name, army, Leadership Value, and MA 10. MAs: German mechanized 10, infantry 7, cavalry 5; Soviet rifle 6, armor 10.
- Untried strengths: rifle divisions range from 0-0-6 to 9-8-6, with about eight 0-0-6 and (per 12.3) two 0-1-6. Tank divisions: three 0-10, three 3-10, one 4-10, two 5-10, one 7-10. Mechanized divisions run 1-10 to 8-10 (three at 8). Simonsen's averages: rifle 3 attack, 4 defense, about 10 percent with zero defense; tank 3, with 30 percent zero; mechanized 5, with none zero. Leader ratings: six at 2, five at 3, two at 4, two at 5 (Koniev, Rokossovski).
- Steps: Soviet units and Leaders have one step (9.62). German mechanized regiments and the cavalry have two (the back side at about half strength, e.g. 4-10 to 2-10, 3-10 to 2-10). German infantry divisions have four (9-7, 4-7, 2-7, 1-7) on two counters (9.63).

## 4. Movement
- Land: MP per the TEC, not saved or transferred (6.2); out of supply, MA is halved, rounded down, minimum 1 (6.25). ZOC is rigid and sticky: units stop on entering it at no extra cost and leave only by combat or Overrun (8.1). Every unit has a ZOC, including Leaders and unrevealed duds; disrupted units do not. Friendly units negate enemy ZOC for supply, leader radius, and retreat, but not for movement (8.15).
- Stacking: 3 combat units per hex for each side, or 4 Soviet if one is a Leader; checked at the end of movement and during combat; any excess is eliminated (7.1-7.2).
- German mechanized movement: all German units move in the Initial Movement Phase. After combat, panzer, mechanized, motorized, and cavalry units move again in the Mechanized Movement Phase, with full MA, a new supply check, and Overruns allowed (4.2, 6.23). At 1/2 MP per road hex, a 10-MA regiment can move 20 road hexes per phase.
- Divisional integration (7.3): if every regiment of one panzer or motorized division is alone in a hex, its strength doubles for attack and defense. Any regiment's elimination ends this for good. A panzer division is 8 points, or 16 integrated. Integration is applied before terrain and before halving (PGG II clarification); infantry and cavalry do not qualify.
- Overrun (6.5): during movement, units starting in one hex move adjacent, pay 3 extra MP, and attack all units in the target hex at half strength, after other adjustments, rounding down. Supply is judged at that instant. If the hex is cleared, they must enter it free and may keep moving or overrun again; a split, Engaged, or adverse result ends their move. There is no advance after combat. Soviet units outside a Leader radius may not overrun.
- Disruption (6.6): an Overrun defender that suffers a loss or retreat (not Engaged) may not move or attack and has no ZOC, but it defends normally. A disrupted Leader neither supplies nor leads. It recovers in its owner's next Disruption Removal Phase, so Soviets disrupted in the German turn are inert through their own next turn's movement and combat.
- Rail (Soviet only, 6.3): up to 8 units per turn (an armored division counts 3; Leaders are free), up to 30 connected rail hexes, start and end on rail, no entry into enemy ZOC; terrain is ignored and supply is not needed. An air interdiction marker costs 4 of the 30 points. Under AH Rail Cut rules, German units cut rail by passing through and Soviet units repair it by entering.
- First turn (5.2): the 16th and 19th Armies each move only on a die roll of 1-3. The 13th and 20th Armies must spend full MA, moving north, south, or east only, may not end in hexrows 0100-0200, and may not use rail.
- Sea: not modeled. Air: no air units; abstracted as interdiction markers (13.0); no airborne units.

## 5. Combat
- Land: voluntary attacks on adjacent units; each unit attacks once and is attacked once per phase; a hex defends as one (9.1-9.2). Soviet units attack only within a Leader's radius (10.35). Odds CRT, 1-3 to 10-1, one die; odds round in the defender's favor; results off the table use the end columns (9.5). Results: Ae/De (all eliminated); A1/A2/D1/D2 (lose that many steps or retreat that many hexes, owner's choice); Eng (both lose one step, no retreat or advance); split results D1/A1, D2/A1, De/A1 (defender applies first).
- Modifiers: forest and major city double; rivers double only if all attackers cross; supply halving comes before terrain (11.31). A stacked Leader adds its rating to an attack only, at most doubling the units' adjusted strength (10.36).
- Retreats (9.7): plotted by the opponent, into vacant hexes first, never into enemy ZOC unless friendly-occupied. Overstacking eliminates. A retreated unit adds no defense to its new hex and dies if that hex later suffers any loss or retreat (9.75).
- Advance (9.8): victors may follow the retreat path, ignoring ZOC; two hexes if the defending hex was wiped out.
- Untried units (12.2-12.3): revealed only as the die is about to be rolled, and committed units may not be withdrawn. A 0-0-6 or 0-10 is removed on revelation, which allows a two-hex advance; a 0-1-6 may only defend.
- Sea and air combat: not modeled.

## 6. Supply
- German (11.1): a unit is in supply within 20 hexes of a road leading unbroken to road hex 0120 (west edge), with path and road free of enemy units, enemy ZOC (unless friendly-occupied), and impassable terrain. Alternatively, it may trace 20 MP (its own type's costs, rivers paid in full) to any west-edge hex (11.12). The Soviet Interdiction marker blocks German supply through its hex; on 0120 it forces the 20-MP method.
- Soviet (11.2): a combat unit traces a Line of Communications no longer, in hexes, than a Leader's rating (2-5) to that Leader. The Leader traces a line of any length to the east edge. Units may never trace straight to the edge. Enemy units, enemy ZOC, lakes, and passage through swamp block both lines; German air interdiction does not. One Leader may supply any number of units.
- Timing: at the start of each movement phase (twice for German mechanized units) and at the instant of each combat or Overrun (11.0). Reinforcements are in supply on their first Player-Turn (11.33).
- Effects (11.3): MA and strength halved, rounded down, minimum 1. There is no attrition and no effect on ZOC. A Soviet unit outside every Leader radius cannot attack but defends at half strength; one within the radius of a cut-off Leader attacks at half strength (10.35).
- Sea and air supply: not modeled.

## 7. Other systems
- Sequence (4.2): Soviet Player-Turn: Movement (supply check, moves, Overruns, reinforcements; German air markers removed at end); Combat; Disruption Removal; Soviet Interdiction. German Player-Turn: Initial Movement; Combat; Mechanized Movement; Disruption Removal; Air Interdiction. The German places his air markers before Turn 1.
- Command: Soviet Leaders supply units, enable attacks, add to attacks, and may absorb a loss (10.0). German command is not modeled beyond integration.
- Interdiction (13.0): three German markers, one per hex, add +1 MP to Soviet entry or cost 4 rail points. They may not go east of hexrow 40xx until one turn after the German holds both Smolensk hexes with a line to the west edge. One Soviet marker is used on any three turns except Turn 12; it has the same movement effect on Germans and blocks German supply.
- Reinforcements (14.0, 16.0): scheduled arrivals may be delayed. German panzer corps arrive on Turns 1-3 (areas A, C, E, H); 18 infantry divisions arrive on Turns 2-9. Soviet armies arrive on Turns 1-11, mostly at hex X. Each turn a die roll places one Provisional rifle division at entry hex 1-6. From Turn 2, up to 5 South-Western Front divisions per turn (10 total) may enter by southern rail hexes at or east of Z, giving the German 1 VP each for the first five and 2 VP each for the next five. All Soviet arrivals are untried and drawn at random by type; there are no replacements.
- Weather: not modeled. Fog of war: untried units only.
- Victory (15.0): German points for cities held at the end, which needs a clear line to the west edge: Vitebsk 10, Orsha 5, Mogilev 5, Smolensk 25, Roslavl 10, Yelnya 10, Rzhev 5, Gzhatsk 5, Vyazma 15, Kaluga 20, hexes 5907 and 5915 20 each. Soviet points: 5 per whole German division destroyed (not cavalry) and 2 per city recapture. Net German total: 0 or less, Soviet Decisive; 1-25 Soviet Strategic; 26-49 Soviet Marginal; 50-79 German Marginal; 80-124 German Strategic; 125 or more German Decisive. Berger's table is misaligned; these bands come from the rule summary.

## 8. Notable observations for a rulebook-outline writer
- The narrative walk-through (2.0) teaches the flow of a turn before the formal cases; it is worth copying.
- Soviet supply and leadership are split between 10.3 and 11.2, with overlapping text; one combined section would read better.
- Key definitions are scattered: the 0-10 armored dud appears in 2.0 and 10.23 but not in 12.3, and the units eligible for the Mechanized Movement Phase are named only in 4.2. Define each unit class once.
- The order of modifiers (integration, terrain, supply, Overrun halving) was settled only in errata; state it once, explicitly.
- Conflicting map charts (Turn 12 reinforcements) argue for a single authoritative schedule.
- Berger's color marking of AH additions and corrections is a good model for recording edition differences.
