# Axis Empires: Dai Senso! (Decision Games, 2011) -- rulebook digest

Source files read: daisenso.txt (Living Rules, February 1, 2014, whole text); daisenso-scen.txt (Living Scenarios, October 1, 2012, whole text, including Designers Notes); "DS Errata October 2012.pdf" (balance notes and corrections); "Dai Senso game map.jpg" (Terrain Effects Chart, Terrain Key and VP Track are printed only on the map, not in the rulebook).

## 0. Rulebook structure
Rulebook contents, in order:
- Introduction: Rulebook Structure; How to Read the Rules; Glossary.
- Game Components: Game Scale; Counters (Units, Markers); Option Cards.
- Core Game Mechanics: Sequence of Play (including the Combined Game Sequence of Play).
  - 0. Seasonal Victory Phase: 0.1 Victory Point Check; 0.2 Automatic Victory; 0.3 V-J Day (End Game) Victory; 0.4 Combined Game Victory.
  - 1. Seasonal Phase: 1.1 Option Card Segment; 1.2 Logistics / Partisan Segment; 1.3 Replacements Segment.
  - 2. Initial Administrative Phase: 2.1 Political Events Segment; 2.2 Support Segment; 2.3 Organization Segment.
  - 3. Operational Movement Phase: 3.1 Movement; 3.2 Overruns.
  - 4. Combat Phase: 4.1 Blitz Combat Segment; 4.2 Regular Combat Segment; 4.3 Marker Segment.
  - 5. Reserve Movement Phase.
  - 6. Final Administrative Phase: 6.1 War & Peace Segment; 6.2 Conditional Events Segment.
  - 7. End of Game Turn Phase: 7.1 Delay Segment; 7.2 Turn Marker Segment.
- Housekeeping Rules: 8. Zone of Control (8.1-8.2); 9. Stacking (9.1-9.3); 10. Supply (10.1-10.3); 11. Weather (11.1 Weather Areas to 11.5 Snow); 12. War State (12.1 Pre-War, 12.2 Limited War, 12.3 Total War Effects); 13. Factions & Countries (13.1 Factions to 13.8 Additional Country Rules).
- Look-Up Rules: 14. Markers (14.1-14.48, one entry per marker, alphabetical); 15. Political Events (15.1-15.37, alphabetical); 16. Conditional Events (16.1-16.6 Axis, Western and Soviet permanent and card events; 16.7 Internment & Repatriation).

Sections 0-7 follow the Sequence of Play exactly, so the rule number equals the phase number. Sections 8-13 hold rules that apply throughout, and 14-16 are reference entries meant to be looked up when a card or event names them. Cases are decimally numbered; gray boxes hold Design Notes, Clarifications and Examples; asterisks and other symbols flag rules that differ in the combined game with Totaler Krieg! (TK). Scenarios, a play-and-learn procedure, and the Designers Notes are in a separate Scenario Book; the CRT and other tables are on player aid cards and the maps, not in the rulebook text.

## 1. Situation and design
- Situation: historical, the Asia-Pacific war from 1937 to V-J Day, with three factions (Axis, Western, Soviet). It is the sister game to TK (Europe); the two combine as Axis Empires (AE). The war in Europe is abstracted in standalone play through the European Strategic Value (ESV) track and European War markers (13.8.5, 14.14).
- Scale: a hex is 120 to 300 miles depending on latitude; a turn is 30 to 60 days, nine turns per year (four Seasonal Turns: Mar-Apr, May-June, Aug-Sept, Nov-Dec). Ground units range from battalions to armies and Chinese War Zones (army groups). One to four players (bidding rules for two, three and four).
- Scenarios: three training scenarios (A.1 August Storm, under one Soviet turn; A.2 The China Incident, 5 turns; A.3 Tora! Tora! Tora!, 4 turns) with special victory conditions; one one-map campaign (B.1 War of Resistance, 1937 until European Limited War, West map only); eight two-map campaigns (C.1 1937 The Rising Sun, "20+ hours"; C.2 1938; C.3 1939; C.4 1940; C.5 1941a When Tigers Fight; C.6 1941b The War in the Pacific, 36 turns; C.7 1942 Waking Giant, 32 turns; C.8 1943 Magic & Vengeance, 23 turns). Campaigns run to V-J Day, which is placed 16 Seasonal Turns after Total War begins (12.3).
- Victory in brief: Strategic Hexes held minus those lost give a Strategic Value that places the VP marker on its Rising Sun or Allied Crusade side (0.1). Automatic victory comes from Asian Domination during Total War (+15 or more, or -17 or less), Japanese Surrender (Allied Crusade 2 VP during Limited War), or ESV reaching 5 (0.2). Otherwise the side with more VPs at the final check wins; ties go to the Axis (0.3).
- Course of play: the scenario notes trace the China Incident becoming a quagmire, the Strike North or South choice of 1941, "victory disease" in 1942 and the 1943 stalemate before US production tells. The rules say the historical final score was a 2-2 tie (Allied Crusade 2 against Japanese Mandate 2). The 2012 errata warns that the Axis wins too easily with ahistorical strategies and recommends counting Soviet Strategic Hexes at half value and making Axis cards 31 and 37 mutually exclusive.
- Variations: bidding for sides in Strategic Hexes; random or chosen Japanese Government at C.1 setup; Strike North or Strike South setup in C.5; the one-map campaign; the AE combined game (Unconditional Surrender bonus VPs, 14.45); variant counters held for an Expansion Kit.
- Key design features (Designers Notes and design notes): a 1937 start so Japan can mire itself in China; option cards drive reinforcements, replacements and politics; the Army/Navy Japanese Government marker models divided leadership; abstract support units keep attention on bases and the big picture; the Naval Warfare Delay Box ("deep six") makes sea losses luck-driven, as at Midway; Policies and Truces force players to act like governments before declaring war (13.6.1.2); seven Chinese minor countries model a divided China.

## 2. Map
- Two 22 x 34 inch maps, West (India) and East (Hawaii); hexes are numbered with a w or e prefix.
- Hex terrain (map TEC): Clear or Desert 1 MP, no shift; City or Capital 1 MP, 1 left; Port 1 MP; Town uses the other terrain; Rough (hills, forest, swamp) 2 MP, 1 left; All-Sea prohibited except by Beachhead. Ice hexes cannot be Naval Bases or Open Ports during North Snow (11.5).
- Hexsides: Mountain +2 MP, 2 left, blocks ZOC; River +1 MP, 1 left; Lake treated like All-Sea; Strait (connected) at road/rail cost, 2 left; Strait (not connected) or Beachhead-2 entire MA, 2 left; Beachhead-1 entire MA, 1 left; SNLF Beachhead-0 entire MA, no shift; All-Sea passable only by Beachhead or marines, marines attack at 2 left. Only the lowest hexside shift applies, added to the hex shift (4.2.3.8).
- Road/Rail: movement along them costs 1/2 MP for one-step and 1 MP for multi-step units and ignores other terrain (3.1.1); supply runs any distance on rail but over only one stretch of road or one connected strait (10.2).
- Other features: Cities and Capitals; Ports, Multi-Zone Ports, Key Ports, five Restricted Waterways; Strategic Hexes (green Western, red Soviet, orange Axis); Oil, Limited Stacking and US Impact hexes; country, dependent and region borders. Naval Zones, each with On Station, Convoys and Used boxes. Off-Map Boxes: Western US, Panama Canal, French Polynesia, Europe/Africa, Eastern Europe. Displays: Turn Track (with printed weather), VP, USCL, Posture and War State displays, Delay and Naval Warfare Delay Boxes, Strategic Warfare and Ceded Lands Boxes; a separate European War Display.
- Markers: Blitz, Beachhead, Convoy, Detachment, Logistics, Partisan Base, Airdrop, Devastation, Policy and Truce, Influence, Ceded Land, Failure, Japanese Government, VP and many Strategic Warfare markers (14.1-14.48). There are no supply or disruption status markers.
- Unique: political geography (countries, dependents, regions, posture) governs where units may go, and control has a three-level priority including Devastation (Glossary).

## 3. Units
- Kinds: ground units (infantry-type including garrison, cavalry, airborne, marine; armor-type; HQ; fortress and port-a-fort), mostly armies, Chinese War Zones and one-step building-block units; support units: Air (Air Force, Bomber, Interceptor, CV Strike) and Fleet (CV Fleet, Surface Fleet, Sub Fleet). Leaders are not modeled. Special classes: colonial (white symbol), elite (shaded), Exp, Kwantung, multi-national and pan-national Chinese HQs (13.8.3).
- Counts: 560 counters on two sheets, plus 76 Axis, 80 Western and 44 Soviet cards. The rules give no per-side unit totals. Estimate from the 1941b setup (C.6): Axis about 56 ground and 6 support units; Western about 55 ground and 8 support; Soviet about 31 ground and 3 support.
- Counter: Attack-Defense-Movement factors (for example 3-3-1 infantry army, 7-6-2 US mech); step value as dots, or squares for garrison, fortress, colonial and Kwantung units; organization size; historical and nationality IDs; delay stripe; range (air, HQ, Blitz, Failure); reinforcement card number or event letter. Detachment and Airdrop markers are printed on the backs of certain infantry and airborne units; support units carry Escort Troop Convoy, Devastation or CV Strike sides.
- Steps: units have one to three steps. Losses flip a unit or swap it for smaller units from the Force Pool; units combine and break down in the Organization Segment, limited absolutely by the Force Pool (2.3, 4.2.6.2).

## 4. Movement
- Land: units must be supplied to start an Operational move (3). Costs come from the TEC; a unit may always enter one hex (minimum move). Units stop on entering an EZOC unless a friendly unit is already there; friendly ground units negate EZOC (8.2). Multi-step armor may overrun a lone one-step unit that would get no defensive shift (3.2). Stacking: three units and six steps, or three steps in Limited Stacking hexes; no two HQs or two fortresses; units of different minor countries may not stack except Exp units (9). Reserve Movement Phase: unsupplied units may move, no EZOC entry or exit, no overruns, and road/rail costs 1 MP per hex in active enemy countries (5). Exploitation after combat requires armor in the attack and cannot exceed MA (4.2.8). Political posture, Pre-War and Kwantung rules restrict entry (3.1.4).
- Sea: Port-to-Port movement uses a friendly Troop Convoy in the zone; each carries one one-step unit, only the US Fleet Train carries a multi-step unit, and debarkation needs an Open Port (3.1.2). Off-Map Box moves (3.1.3). Amphibious: Surface Fleets (must) and Air Forces (may) placed in All-Sea hexes become Beachhead markers (2.2.3.4); units may enter or land from them, and units left at sea without a Beachhead are eliminated (2.2.5). Marines cross All-Sea hexsides.
- Air: support units are placed, not moved, each Support Segment. Air Force within 3 hexes of an Air Base; Bomber within 9; CV Strike within 1 hex of its CV Fleet; paths may not enter Mud hexes or neutral or policy-affected countries (2.2.3.1). Airdrop places airborne units as markers within 2 hexes (4.1.1).

## 5. Combat
- Land: the phasing faction may attack adjacent hexes; each hex once per segment, all defenders involved; attackers need not be supplied (4.2.1). Odds CRT, 1-3 to 9-1, one six-sided die, fractions in the defender's favor. Shifts: +1 per friendly Air unit in or adjacent to the defending hex; +1 for a supplied HQ (which can lend its factors from 2 hexes as Ranged Support); in Blitz Combat only, +1 for armor and +1 per Airdrop; +1 for marines; defender gets fortress, HQ, Mud and terrain shifts (4.2.3). Results: Ad, Ex, Dr1-Dr3 retreats and attacker/defender step-loss pairs; retreats come before attrition, and bad weather reduces Dr results by one level (4.2.4, 11.2). Blitz Combat is a second, earlier combat segment for units within 2 hexes of a card-granted Blitz marker; it is barred in Mud, Storms and Snow, except Russians in Snow (4.1). Retreat priorities, HQ and 0-MA units cannot retreat, stacks retreated into are swept up, and cities without a Blitz marker allow voluntary conversion of retreats to losses (4.2.5). Armor or marine steps must be lost first (4.2.6.1).
- Sea: no ship-to-ship combat. Contested placement sends both units to the Delay Box (air) or the Naval Warfare Delay Box (fleet), where a 6 is rerolled and added (2.2.3.2, 7.1). Fleets and air units in On Station boxes deny enemy Open Ports, which cuts transport and supply.
- Air: Air units give combat shifts, deny the enemy Air and Naval Bases, combination and replacement in their hex, and block airdrops nearby. Bombers make permanent Devastation (control) or sit in the Strategic Warfare Box to change enemy Delay DRMs, as do Sub Fleets. The A-bomb is an event (15.33).

## 6. Supply
- Land: sources are Home Country Cities, Partisan Bases (for their minor country only), and Western or Soviet Off-Map Boxes (10.1). Trace from the unit: two free hexes, then any distance on connected rail, one stretch of road or connected strait, and across Naval Zones between Open Ports where a friendly Supply Convoy sits (10.2). Lines may not enter EZOC hexes without a friendly unit, enemy units or markers, enemy cities not controlled, neutral countries, or (for Allies at war) policy-affected hexes. Unsupplied units cannot start Operational moves, combine, serve as bases, give HQ support, airdrop, advance or exploit; they may still attack, defend, move in Reserve Movement, break down and convert to fortresses (10.3). There is no attrition or elimination for lack of supply; isolation has no separate rule.
- Sea: Convoy markers (Standard, Scratch, Fleet Train, Escort) placed in Convoys boxes in Troop or Supply mode (2.2.3.6); shipping losses are abstracted into Delay DRMs and events.
- Air: air supply is not modeled.

## 7. Other systems
- Sequence: Seasonal Victory Phase, then Axis, Western and Soviet Faction Turns, each running Phases 1-6 as listed in section 0; then the End of Game Turn Phase (Delay rolls, turn marker).
- Command: HQ Ranged Support; the Japanese Government marker restricts card choice and Blitz placement (Cabinet Crisis Table) (14.22); Failure markers impair a faction within 5 hexes (14.15).
- Reinforcements and replacements: Option cards are chosen a season ahead and revealed face up; they add units to the Force Pool, Delay Box or Strategic Warfare Box and grant seasonal and conditional replacement steps; there are extra steps from Hakko Ichiu, Japanese Mandate, minor production and the US Victory Program (1.1-1.3). Units with delay stripes return from the Delay Box after d6 turns plus DRMs (7.1).
- Politics: War States (Pre-War, Limited, Total) limit cards and actions (12); Posture, Policies and Truces govern each Allied country (13.6); minor countries activate by events or declaration (6.1, 13.7); USCL and ESV tracks.
- Weather: fixed by calendar on the Turn Track for seven named weather areas (the text says six but lists seven); Mud, Storms and Snow (11).
- Fog of war: Pending cards are face down and hands are secret; units are open.
- Victory: see section 1 and 0.1-0.4.

## 8. Notable observations for a rulebook-outline writer
- Numbering the core rules to match the Sequence of Play (Phase 4 is Section 4) makes "read as you play" learning practical; it is worth copying.
- The three-part split into core mechanics, housekeeping and alphabetical look-up entries keeps event text out of the main flow, but some definitions a player needs early (Air Base, Open Port, Naval Base) live only in the Glossary, and 2.2 has to send the reader back to it.
- The "How to Read the Rules" section defines a, one, may, must and No Result strictly; this is a good model for removing ambiguity.
- Key charts (TEC, VP Track, CRT, Delay DRMs) appear only on maps and aid cards, so the rulebook alone is incomplete; a new rulebook should reproduce or at least summarize them.
- The support-unit section is dense and the designers admit it is opaque; separate placement, contest and convoy rules with a one-page summary, as the aid card does.
