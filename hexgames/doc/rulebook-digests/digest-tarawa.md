# D-Day at Tarawa -- rulebook digest

Source files read: tarawa.txt (Rules Booklet V1.3, Decision Games 2017, design John Butterfield); tarawa-add.txt (Addenda, rev. 11/16/16). Consulted: DDaT_Panel.pdf (player-aid charts); USMC OOB Chart V1.1; DDaT_Flipbook_v1.3.pdf (fan aid, used only for the Terrain Effects Chart); tarawa_positions.pdf; map image DDaT-map1.png.

## 0. Rulebook structure
1.0 Introduction. 2.0 Game Components (2.1 Map; 2.2 Playing Pieces; 2.3 Cards; 2.4 Charts & Tables). 3.0 Setting Up for Play. 4.0 Sequence of Play. 5.0 US Amphibious Operations (5.1 Landing by LVT; 5.2 Wading Ashore; 5.3 Placing Units in Beach Approach Hexes; 5.4 Landing US Tanks). 6.0 Japanese Fire (6.1 Fire Cards; 6.2 Fields of Fire; 6.3 Resolving Fire; 6.4 Disrupted Japanese Units; 6.5 Japanese Artillery Fire). 7.0 US Actions (7.1 Free Actions; 7.2 Conducting Actions; 7.3 Move; 7.4 Enter Enemy Occupied Hex; 7.5 Stacking; 7.6 Disrupted US Units). 8.0 US Combat Actions (8.1 Eligibility; 8.2 Attack Weapons; 8.3 Resolving an Attack; 8.4 Barrage; 8.5 Naval Fire Markers; 8.6 Close Combat). 9.0 Japanese Units, Depth & Reserves (9.1-9.3). 10.0 US Heroes & Headquarters (10.1-10.3). 11.0 Control & Communication (11.1-11.3). 12.0 Japanese Lettered Actions (12.1-12.10). 13.0 Japanese Tank Units. 14.0 Winning & Losing the First Waves. 15.0 Extended Turns. 16.0 Command Posts. 17.0 US Engineer Support Markers. 18.0 Winning & Losing the Extended Game. 19.0 The Overnight Turn. 20.0 21 November Scenario. 21.0 Optional Rules. Appendices: Event Descriptions; bibliography; Japanese Fire/Action Summary.

The book follows the sequence of play: landing, Japanese fire, US actions and combat, then the Japanese machinery (depth, communication, lettered actions, tanks). Sections 1-14 are a complete basic game (Turns 1-10); 15-19 add extended-game rules; 20-21 give a scenario and optional rules. Cases are decimal, up to three levels. Illustrated examples are set off in boxes; most charts are on separate player aids.

## 1. Situation and design
- Situation: historical. The 2nd Marine Division's assault on Betio, Tarawa Atoll, 20-21 November 1943, against about 5,000 Japanese troops and Korean laborers (1.0). Solitaire: the player is the US; cards and fixed priorities run the Japanese.
- Scale: 100-yard hexes (2.1). Turns 1-10 are 30 minutes, Turns 11-30 one hour, and Turn 16 an 11-hour night turn (1.0, 19.0). US units are companies and batteries plus three regimental HQs; Japanese units are detachments of four formations. One player.
- Scenarios (3.0, 20.0): The First Waves (Turns 1-10, about 3 hours, rules 1-14 only); 20 November 1943 (Turns 1-15, 4 hours); Two Days in Hell (Turns 1-30, 7 hours); 21 November 1943 (Turns 17-30, 4 hours, own setup). Victory counts "secured" Japanese position hexes: US-occupied, every position projecting intense fire into it also occupied, and in US communication (14.2). Thresholds: 10 by Turn 10; 15 by Turn 15, one projecting fire onto the southern beach (18.1 as corrected); Two Days in Hell checks at Turn 15 (20 secured is a decisive win, under 12 a decisive loss), else at Turn 30 needs all positions in three zones, one of them A or F, plus eight more (18.2). Catastrophic loss ends any scenario as a loss (14.1).
- History and play: the rules say only that the Marines met unexpectedly strong resistance and heavy losses; brief historical notes cover Major Ryan's isolated western landing (10.17), the LVT shortage, the undelivered order to land 1/8 Marines on Day 1, and Shibasaki's death on the first afternoon (21.3). Typical play outcomes are not described.
- Optional rules (21.0): Hawkins' Raid (three fire cards decide whether the pier raid succeeds; failure adds depth at D2 and E2); free choice of beach for 1/8 Marines; Admiral Shibasaki Survives (his death events cancelled, US reserves accelerated, but victory requires eliminating every Japanese unit).
- Key design features: (a) fixed, color-coded Japanese positions with printed fields of fire, activated by colors drawn on a card, giving an automatic but unpredictable defense; (b) hidden strength via face-down units and depth markers revealed only under attack; (c) weapon matching, which the only design note (8.2) ranks with numerical strength; (d) Japanese lettered actions that unlock over time (12.0); (e) leaders who grant free actions beyond the three-action limit. There are no designer's notes; the campaign article was not available.

## 2. Map
- Terrain (Terrain Effects Chart): Beach, Clear, Airstrip: no effect. Palm Trees: US infantry ranged fire through the hex is halved (addenda). Building: Japanese unit strength doubled, depth marker not. Fortified Building: unit and depth doubled. Rough/Crater: unit doubled, depth not; non-infantry US units (tanks, artillery) must stop on entry. Benefits do not combine; Japanese tanks get none (13.3); terrain does not affect close combat (8.6). Water hexes (open water, beach approach, pier or wreck hexes) are open to US units; a beach hex is both water and land (5.2).
- Linear features: Seawall hexside doubles unit (not depth) strength if every adjacent attacker attacks across it. Pier hexsides block wading (5.22). The coral reef hexside and the colored water fire-arc boundary lines are printed; the reef has no movement rule of its own (boats simply unload there, 5.1). No roads, rails or rivers.
- Other features: Japanese position hexes, each with zone letter A-F, priority number 1-17, one of six colors, and setup marks (C coastal, I inland, artillery class L/M/H, armor symbol) (2.1). The position aid lists about 85 positions (A14, B12, C14, D17, E15, F13; my count). Positions in or adjacent to a beach hex are coastal; others inland. Linked same-color positions form position groups that fire as one. US beach approach hexes on the north and east edges (Red 1-3, A-D, and Green), each with an arrow for the run in; LVT wreck hexes; the Niminoa wreck (no effect). Tracks and boxes: Turn, Card/Phase, Japanese Reserve, Coastal and Inland Depth, Japanese Allowed Actions, Japanese Eliminated, Shibasaki, US Infantry Loss, Command Post range, Available LVTs.
- Markers: Turn, Phase, action letters, Hero/Inspired, Action Taken, CP range, Support/Garrison, Naval Fire (strength 9), Disrupted (US in two shades, 7.6), Artillery Destroyed, Smoke, No LVT Communication, Shibasaki.
- Unique: intense and steady fire dots around each position; steady-fire water arcs for north- and west-shore coastal positions (6.23); special landing placement at the Red 1 "bird's beak" (5.12).

## 3. Units
- Kinds: US rifle and heavy (D, H, M) infantry companies, engineers (flamethrower FT or support SP), tanks (armored), artillery, and HQs; LVTs are markers, not units (2.22); 8 hero markers including Major Ryan (10.18). Japanese: infantry, heavy infantry, engineer, HQ, antitank, machine gun and tank, all identical in play except tanks (2.24). No air or naval units.
- Counts: 352 counters. Japanese: 46 units (28 coastal, 3 armor, 15 others: 11 inland, 4 reserve) (3.0); total depth markers not stated (they include 3 armor and 4 coastal tactical-reinforcement markers, 20.1). US, estimated from the OOB chart: 28 rifle companies (12 each of 2nd and 8th Marines, 4 of 1/6), about 12 engineer units, 7 tank companies, 3 HQs, 3 event-only artillery batteries, and 5 "D+2" units used only with 21.3; plus 9 LVTs.
- US counter: designation, arrival turn and beach, type, attack strength, steps, range (number or U), target symbol; reduced infantry also print their remaining weapons (2.21). OOB examples: rifle 7-2, heavy 8-3, tank 4-7, artillery 4-U, engineer 2. Full-strength weapons are on the US Weapons Chart (BZ, MG, BR, MO, RD, DE, FT, AR, NA). Japanese counter: formation, defense strength, US weapon requirements (including FL flanking and CC close combat), coastal code, and for tanks a position color; the back shows only general type (2.23). Elite units (7SSNLF, 3SBDF, 111th Pioneers HQ) have a red symbol.
- Steps: rifle companies have 4 steps on two counters; other US units 1-2; HQs none; LVTs 2. Japanese units have no steps: they are revealed, disrupted, defeated, or gain and lose depth markers.

## 4. Movement
- Land: no movement points; a move action is up to 2 land hexes (3 from Turn 11) (7.3, 15.31). A unit stops on entering an intense-fire hex of a non-disrupted position or any hex adjacent to an enemy unit (7.31), which serves as the zone of control. Infiltration between two hexes in the same position's field of fire draws a fire card; a step is lost if it shows that color and the unit's target symbol (7.32). Entering an enemy hex is a separate action leading to close combat (7.4). Stacking: 2 US units per hex at the end of the Action Phase; leaders, garrisons and LVTs are free; 5+ steps make a "concentrated target" (7.5, 6.35). No strategic movement, retreat or advance after combat (8.34). Japanese units move only by lettered actions.
- Sea (amphibious, 5.0): Next turn's arrivals are placed in approach hexes A-C of their beach (D opens on Turn 5 for R2 and R3), two per hex, facing the printed arrow (5.3). In the Amphibious Operations Phase:
  1. Assign LVTs to non-tank units: a full LVT carries up to 4 steps in one or two units; a reduced LVT one 2-step unit; two 1-step LVTs may combine; HQs ride free (5.11). The 9 LVTs suffice only on Turn 1.
  2. Landing check, west to east, two cards per LVT or tank (5.12). Card 1, drift: none, 1L, 1R, or PR (pivot to a secondary arrow, Red 2 only). Card 2: U# (steps the cargo keeps), L# (steps the LVT keeps; X loses all), and location: Reef (stay in approach hex), Water (run on to the next LVT-wreck hex), Beach, or Inland 1/2 (one or two hexes past the beach, ignoring Japanese units; an occupied hex means close combat). Red 1 runs zig-zag; bird's-beak landings go to 1338 or 1437. HQs may ignore drift, may treat Inland as Beach, and never die in landing (5.13). Tanks swim in by the same check, treating Reef and Water as Beach (5.4).
  3. Every unit in water not carried this phase must wade 2 hexes (3 from Turn 11) toward the beach (5.2, 5.21). These represent troops that the unseen boats left at the reef (5.1).
  4. Surviving LVTs return to the box (5.14).

  Green Beach (Turn 27, longer scenarios) has no LVTs or landing checks; all units wade, under water fire (15.4). Tides are not mentioned anywhere; the reef that boats cannot cross is the only expression of water depth. Events interfere: No ship-to-shore communication, Transport delayed, LVT commandeered for wounded, Marines hitch a ride.
- Air: not modeled.

## 5. Combat
- Japanese fire (6.0): one fire card per turn shows three position colors, a target symbol, and possibly stars (leader hits), armor bonus, action letters, artillery value. A single color symbol activates any occupied non-disrupted position; a double symbol needs unit plus depth or two units (6.3). The Japanese Fire Chart crosses dot type with revealed or unrevealed firer: intense fire costs every unit a step (plus disruption if unrevealed); steady fire costs a step to non-armored units with the target symbol (plus disruption on land if unrevealed); machine-gun fire (M) reaches hexes adjacent to intense hexes. Hit limit: one US unit per Japanese unit and depth marker (plus one from Turn 11), by priority intense, steady, MG, most steps, nearest (6.31, 15.2). Turns 1-10: at most one step lost per US unit per phase (6.34). Artillery (from Turn 2): if the card's required number of occupied artillery positions is met (e.g., 8 of any type, or 2 heavy), one unit with the target symbol loses a step, chosen in approach hexes first, then water, then beach (6.5); US occupation or naval fire destroys artillery permanently (6.51).
- US attack (8.1-8.3): needs one adjacent infantry or engineer unit; ranged infantry (rifle range 2, heavy 3), tanks (range 7, with adjacency or HQ conditions) and artillery (in HQ command, not in a field of fire) may join; each participant spends an action; one attack per hex per phase. Reveal the unit (not its depth); total US strength versus terrain-modified defense; check whether all required weapons are present; read the US Attack Results Chart (rows: weapons yes/no by less, equal, greater, double; columns: unit alone, with unrevealed depth, with revealed depth). Results: attackers disrupted, Japanese gain depth, Japanese disrupted, reveal depth and re-read, depth eliminated, defeated. FL needs attacks from two non-adjacent hexes (three if doubled); CC is met only by close combat; a hero or support marker supplies one other weapon; HQ command supplies a radio (8.22-8.25). Defeated elite units in communication withdraw to reserve on Turns 1-16 (8.33).
- Barrage (8.4, 15.32): one tank (artillery from Turn 11), in range but not adjacent, with an observer in the target's field of fire; a fire card read on the US Barrage Table (color, symbol, depth, strength 2 or 4) can at best disrupt. Unrevealed inland units are immune. Naval fire markers (from events) add strength 9 plus NA/AR/DE to an attack, or make a free barrage that disrupts and strips depth (8.5).
- Close combat (8.6): on landing into an occupied hex, on Japanese Assault or tank Overrun, and after US entry. Piles: US one card per step (max 4) plus one each for flamethrower and hero; Japanese one per unit and depth marker, plus one each for attacking, strength 4+, each CC requirement, and tank or night. Cards alternate, Japanese first; a card in the hex's color costs the other side a step (or a depth marker, then the unit); some cards carry events. Ends by elimination, exhaustion (US returns adjacent, disrupted) or US withdrawal (8.61-8.63).
- Sea and air combat: not modeled.

## 6. Supply
- Land: none as such; communication substitutes. Japanese communication (11.2): a path of any length, not through US-controlled hexes or beach hexes, to at least two other occupied positions or groups; required for depth, reserves, withdrawal and some actions. US control is the unit's hex (1-step infantry, engineers, artillery, HQs, CPs, garrisons) or also the six adjacent hexes (infantry with 2+ steps, tanks) (11.1). US communication (11.3) traces to a beachhead beach hex avoiding Japanese units and fields of fire; it is needed to secure positions and to use support markers. Japanese Re-Supply adds depth (12.1); depth pools can run dry (9.23).
- Sea: no shipping system; arrival schedule, LVT supply and events stand in.
- Air: not modeled.

## 7. Other systems
- Sequence (4.0): I. Amphibious Operations; II. Event (Turn 1 automatically adds depth to two units); III. Japanese Fire (colors left to right, positions west to east; artillery; remove disruption for drawn colors); IV. US Action (3 actions plus free ones, then close combat); V. End of Turn. From Turn 11 a second Event Phase and a US Engineer and HQ Phase (support markers, garrisons, command posts) are added (15.1).
- Command: free actions for units with hero or inspired markers, disrupted units (only to remove disruption), HQs and units in or adjacent to them, units within CP range (to 5 hexes, 16.0), and units given a support marker (17.4). Leaders are hit only by "star" colors (10.3).
- Japanese system: action chits R, M, A, P are turned on Turns 3, 5, 7, 9 in random order, I on Turn 11 (12.0). The Fire/Action Summary crosses card letter with position situation to yield fire, Re-Supply, Redeploy, Reinforce, Mortar, Muster, Patrol, Artillery Fire, Assault, Ambush or Infiltrate; tanks have their own menu (13.2).
- Reinforcements: fixed US schedule with event changes; Japanese reserves enter by actions, tactical-reinforcement depth markers (9.3), Tunnels!, and night reorganization (19.0).
- Night (Turn 16): fields of fire shrink to 1 hex; a living Shibasaki gives two Fire Phases; US moves 1 hex with no ranged or naval fire; revealed Japanese units flip face down. No weather.
- Fog of war: random face-down setup, hidden depth, random action-letter order.

## 8. Notable observations for a rulebook-outline writer
- The amphibious procedure is a model: a numbered phase summary, one case per step, then a worked example for a whole beach.
- Much rule content lives in map printing (fire dots, arcs, connectors) and the Fire/Action Summary; the booklet is short but the Terrain Effects Chart is absent from it.
- Cross-references are unreliable (leader hits cited as 11.4 instead of 10.3; CP range cited as 18.1; "US Operations Phase" in 16.21; no 8.26 in the contents), and the addenda fix substantive errors (12.5, 14.2, 18.1, 18.2). Verify every reference.
- Catastrophic loss (14.1) states two different tests; give one.
- The basic/extended split, with a scenario using only basic rules, teaches well, but "Starting Turn 11" clauses are scattered through basic cases; a single table of Turn 11 changes would help.
- Without designer's notes, the reasons for abstractions (no tide variable, no boat units) go unstated.
