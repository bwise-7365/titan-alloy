## Choices and results (worker fills in: method, exceptions, before/after counts)

Tools (both carry the copyright line first and last):
- `map_graphics/xml/tools/network_check.py SHEET... [--quiet]` prints every piece of every hexside line and link
  kind (size, ends) and every broken acceptance rule; exit 1 if any. It snaps hex corners with a small tolerance
  index: a plain `round()` split TRC's corner row between rows M and N in two (float noise at y*2 = k+0.5), which
  made the whole map look like two islands to a river search. ctest `hygiene_map_networks` (label hygiene) runs it
  on both sheets, beside the xsd entries in the top CMakeLists.txt.
- `map_graphics/xml/tools/tidy_networks.py SHEET [--dry-run]` rewrites only the kinds network_check finds broken,
  so a second run is a no-op (checked on both sheets: "nothing to do", files byte-identical). No randomness.
  - Rivers, on the hexside-vertex graph: each authored guide (source-to-mouth hex ids, per sheet in `SHEETS`) is
    routed along land-land hexsides near its centre line (cost grows with distance squared), with a penalty for
    two same-way turns (curling round a hex), not touching an earlier river except at the mouth. Then cycles are
    broken (spanning tree from an outlet), runs of 4-5 sides round one hex are replaced by the 2-1 on its far
    side, hairs of <= 2 sides off a junction pruned, short pieces joined within reach, dangling pieces run to
    sea/lake/map edge/another river, and anything still short or undrained dropped.
  - Links, on the hex graph: terminals are the named places (rail: city-major/city/city-minor/capital; road:
    every named place) plus the map-edge end of each traced piece of >= 8 hexes; a Takahashi-Matsuyama Steiner
    heuristic joins them one at a time (sea, lake, blocked hexsides impassable; woods 1.8, swamp 2.2,
    mountain 2.8; traced steps x0.3, traced hexes x0.6; 60/120-degree turns +0.15/+1.5; river crossing +0.7;
    running beside the network +0.4); then up to 6 (TRC) / 4 (PGG) direct trunk lines between major cities
    whose network route is > 1.3x (PGG 1.2x) the direct one. Written as maximal chains (`<link>` of many hexes).
  - Output keeps each surviving river hexside's original `at=` spelling; river edges go where the first river
    edge stood, links where the first link of the kind stood; the `<!-- N edges/links -->` counts are updated
    and the two "traced from the scan" comments say the tool tidied them. Nothing else in either sheet changed
    (git diff: only `<edge line="river">`, `<link>` and those comment lines).
- Why authored river guides: PGG's 1451 traced "river" hexsides are the scan's blue hex grid (a regular pattern
  across the whole sheet), useless as hints; TRC's are short coastline-hugging fragments. PGG guides were read off
  `PGG - low res.png` through an affine fit on the 13 city hexes (fit error up to about one hex), 14 rivers
  (Dnieper, W. Dvina, Mezha, Vop, Kasplya, Sozh, Ostyor, Desna, Ugra, Oka, Volga, Gzhat, one unnamed, Bolva);
  TRC's 21 guides follow real geography anchored on the sheet's cities (Dnieper, Desna, Pripet, Don, Donets,
  Volga in three reaches, Oka, Moskva, W. Dvina, Neman, Vistula, Oder, Dniester, S. Bug, Danube, Prut, Volkhov,
  N. Dvina, Kuban). TRC also keeps traced pieces of >= 10 sides that no guide touches and prefers traced sides.
- Exceptions (in the checker): a link may enter a water hex that holds a named place. TRC's Riga F17, Helsinki
  C14 and Sevastopol KK23 are classified sea (known, see the scenario notes); rail reaches them as line ends and
  nothing routes through them. No separate rail systems: every sheet's rail is one piece. TRC's traced rail end
  at A23 is cut off by blocked hexsides and was left out (the tool prints it).
